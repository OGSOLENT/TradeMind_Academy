## The daily anchor points

Every trading day is framed by three levels from yesterday. Mark them before the session opens.

| Anchor | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **PDH** | Previous Day High |
| **PDL** | Previous Day Low |
| **EQ** | Equilibrium — the 50% midpoint between them |

PDH and PDL are the liquidity pools from Lesson 3: the most-watched intraday levels, with stops stacked on both sides. EQ is the fair-value midpoint that tells you which half of yesterday's range price is trading in.

Bias comes from how price behaves *relative to these three lines* — not from a feeling about direction.

## Continuation anatomy

How a bullish trend day builds, in four beats:

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1. Mark levels** | Mark the EQ and PDH boundaries |
| **2. Wait for the open** | Wait for the market to open (9:30 for US indices) |
| **3. Respect EQ** | Watch price trade lower and *respect* the EQ — holding above it |
| **4. Break PDH** | Price trades up and through the PDH — buyers in control, bullish bias |

Step 3 is the tell. In a genuine bullish day, price may dip — but it holds at or above equilibrium. Respecting EQ says buyers are defending the midpoint. Once PDH breaks, that bias is confirmed.

### The impatient trader

The video singles out the trader who acts at 9:30 before any of this has happened. There is no information at the open — only volatility. The sequence needs time to reveal itself, and every step above requires a *completed* observation, not an anticipated one.

## Spotting reversals

When the trend fails, the sequence is different:

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1. Open extreme** | Price opens directly into the Previous Day High or Low |
| **2. Shallow wick** | A shallow wick forms upon rejection |
| **3. Confirm shift** | Confirm a lower-timeframe shift in structure |
| **4. Trade to EQ** | Trade back into the range, targeting the EQ |

Opening straight into PDH or PDL is the setup: price begins the day at a liquidity pool. If it rejects — shallow wick, then a confirmed lower-timeframe shift (your CISD from Lesson 7) — the day is likely to reverse back into the range, and EQ is the natural first target.

## Confirming the setup

> **How do we know a reversal is valid?**

By now the answer should be automatic: not by the shape, but by the lower-timeframe confirmation. Volume, order flow and candle appearance all help, but the mechanical proof is the structure shift.

## Across timeframes — the fractal market

Bias and execution are paired. Each bias timeframe drills down to a matching execution timeframe:

| Bias | Execution |
|---|---|
| **1 Day** | 1-Hour |
| **4 Hours** | 15-Minute |
| **1 Hour** | 5-Minute |

The pattern is roughly a 1:4 step down. Use the bias chart to decide *what* you are doing; use the execution chart to decide *when*.

Going too far down without a bias — straight to the 1-minute — gives you noise. Staying too high gives you no entry. The pairing exists to keep both.

## Emotions or mechanics?

The lesson closes with the question the whole module is built around:

> **Will you trust emotions or the mechanics?**

The anchor points are mechanical: PDH, PDL, EQ are the same three prices for everyone, computable before the market opens. Bias derived from how price interacts with them is checkable. Bias derived from how the chart *feels* is not.

## Key terms

- **PDH / PDL** — previous day high / previous day low
- **EQ (Equilibrium)** — the 50% midpoint of a range
- **Continuation** — price respects EQ and breaks the prior extreme
- **Reversal** — price opens into an extreme, rejects, and returns to EQ
- **Bias timeframe / execution timeframe** — the higher/lower pairing

## Common mistakes

- **Trading the open.** The first minutes contain volatility, not information.
- **Deciding bias before the levels are marked.** The levels come first, always.
- **Mismatching timeframes.** A daily bias executed on the 1-minute chart is a daily idea drowned in noise.
