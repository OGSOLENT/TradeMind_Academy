## The question this lesson answers

Lesson 7a separated fair value gaps from ordinary market gaps and told you not to mark weekend gaps as setups. That's still true. But the market's opening gaps aren't useless. They're a different kind of array, and this lesson is how to use them.

## Where the gaps come from

Futures markets don't trade continuously. There's a break of about an hour every weekday evening in New York, and a longer break over the weekend. Price when trading stops and price when it resumes are usually different. The space between is an opening gap.

> **New Day Opening Gap (NDOG).** The gap between the 5pm New York close and the 6pm reopen, Monday to Thursday.

> **New Week Opening Gap (NWOG).** The gap between Friday's close and Sunday's reopen.

They're both "no trading happened here" gaps, the standard gap from Lesson 7a's comparison table. What makes them useful is that the algorithm tends to treat that empty space as unfinished business.

## Why they act as magnets

An opening gap is a span of prices where no orders were ever matched. The market has a strong tendency to revisit those spans, either to fill them completely or to trade back to their midpoint before continuing. So an opening gap above price becomes a plausible draw, a place price may be delivered to, in the same way an old high is.

Two behaviours to expect:

1. **Fill.** Price returns into the gap, often to its 50%, the consequent encroachment, and reacts there.
2. **Support and resistance.** Once filled, the gap's edges keep acting as levels. A bearish opening gap (open below close) that's been filled will tend to hold price up on later returns.

## Marking them

For each gap, mark three lines:

| Line | What it is |
|---|---|
| Top | The higher of the close and the open |
| Bottom | The lower of the two |
| CE | The 50% between them |

Most traders keep the last few NDOGs and the last three or four NWOGs on the chart, and delete older ones as they get fully traded through. Some opening gaps are tiny and can be ignored. A gap of a few ticks isn't a magnet.

## Using them

Opening gaps are mostly used in two ways:

- **As a target.** If your bias is bullish and there's an unfilled NWOG overhead, that's a reasonable first target, especially its CE.
- **As a level to react at.** If price retraces into a filled NWOG in the discount half of your range, it's an array like any other, and you look for the confirmation there.

They're weaker than a fresh fair value gap in a displacement, so in the matrix from Lesson 29 they rank as supporting evidence rather than the main reason for a trade. But because they're anchored to the clock rather than to structure, they give you levels on days when the structure is messy.

## The weekly one is heavier

The NWOG carries more weight than the NDOG because the weekend gap tends to be larger and because the new week's opening price, like the Monday range from Lesson 21, is a reference the whole week keeps coming back to. If you only mark one type, mark the NWOG.

---

## Key terms

- **NDOG**: the new day opening gap, 5pm to 6pm New York
- **NWOG**: the new week opening gap, Friday close to Sunday open
- **Consequent encroachment (CE)**: the 50% of a gap
- **Fill**: price trading back into an opening gap

## Common mistakes

- **Marking opening gaps as fair value gaps.** They're a different array with less weight.
- **Keeping every gap forever.** Delete the ones that have been traded through.
- **Marking tiny gaps.** A few ticks isn't a magnet.
- **Making the gap the whole trade.** It's a target or a supporting level, not the setup.
