## The question this lesson answers

Lesson 12 gave you equilibrium, the 50% of a range. This lesson turns that into a complete way of pricing every entry: is this a good price, or an expensive one?

## The dealing range

Take the last clear expansion leg, from its swing low to its swing high. That's your dealing range. Split it in half.

> **Above the 50% level the range is in premium. Below it, discount. The 50% itself is equilibrium.**

Premium and discount are just what they sound like. In a bullish leg, buying in discount means buying below fair value. Buying in premium means paying up. The market has a habit of punishing people who pay up, because premium in a bullish leg is exactly where the sellers who missed the top are waiting.

| Where price is | For a long | For a short |
|---|---|---|
| Premium (above 50%) | Expensive. Don't. | Good. Look for the setup here. |
| Equilibrium (50%) | Fair. Acceptable with confluence. | Fair. Acceptable with confluence. |
| Discount (below 50%) | Good. Look for the setup here. | Expensive. Don't. |

That table is the whole discipline. Most bad trades are the right direction at the wrong price.

## The Optimal Trade Entry

Inside discount there's a narrower band that ICT singles out.

> **Optimal Trade Entry (OTE).** The retracement between 61.8% and 79% of the leg, with 70.5% as the marked midpoint.

Draw a Fibonacci retracement across the leg, from the low to the high for a long. The 0.618, 0.705 and 0.79 levels are your band. The idea is simple: it's deep enough that your stop, just beyond the swing low, is close, and it's early enough that the continuation of the leg is still ahead of you. That combination is what makes the risk-to-reward attractive. A stop below the low and a target at the high of the leg is already better than 2 to 1 from the middle of the band.

Note the number 70.5 isn't a Fibonacci ratio. It's the halfway point between 61.8 and 79. It's marked because it's the middle of the band, not because of any special maths.

## What OTE isn't

The ratios have no force of their own. Price is under no obligation to reach the band, and no obligation to hold there when it does. OTE is a place to look for evidence, not evidence itself. So the band only becomes an entry when something from the earlier modules lines up inside it:

- A fair value gap from the displacement sits in the band. This is the common one.
- An order block sits in the band.
- Price sweeps a small low inside the band and shifts structure on the lower timeframe.

If the band is empty, with no gap and no block, it's just a Fibonacci level, and Fibonacci levels on their own are not a plan.

## Putting it together

A bullish example, step by step:

1. Daily bias is bullish (Module 8).
2. Price expands up on the 15-minute chart, leaving a fair value gap in the leg.
3. You draw the range from the leg's low to its high. The gap sits at roughly 68% of the retracement. It's in the band, and it's in discount.
4. Price retraces into the gap during a kill zone.
5. You enter in the gap, stop below the swing low, first target at the high of the leg.

Every piece of that came from a different lesson. OTE is the one that told you the price was right.

## The mirror for shorts

Everything flips. Draw the leg from the high down to the low. Premium is the upper half, and the OTE band is 62% to 79% back up into it. You sell into premium, stop above the swing high, target the low.

## Key terms

- **Dealing range**: the last clear expansion leg, low to high
- **Premium / discount**: above / below the 50% of the range
- **Equilibrium**: the 50% level itself
- **OTE**: the 61.8% to 79% retracement band, 70.5% marked
- **Confluence**: a gap or block sitting inside the band

## Common mistakes

- **Buying in premium because the bias is bullish.** Direction right, price wrong.
- **Entering on the Fibonacci level alone.** The band needs a gap or a block inside it.
- **Drawing the range across the wrong leg.** Use the most recent clear expansion, not the whole week.
- **Ignoring equilibrium.** The 50% is the first place a retracement can end. If you're waiting for OTE, know you may not get it.
