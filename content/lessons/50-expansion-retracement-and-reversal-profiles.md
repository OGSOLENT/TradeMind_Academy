## Three profiles

Every higher-timeframe candle you'll ever read fits one of three profiles. Learning to name the profile while the candle is still open is most of the skill in this model, because the profile decides what you expect from the candle after it.

> **Expansion.** Small wicks on both ends, a large body, clear momentum. The candle opened, went, and closed near its extreme.

> **Retracement.** A modest body against the previous direction, wicks on both ends, no displacement. The candle gave back part of the prior move and stopped.

> **Reversal.** Opens, makes a large opposing run that leaves a long wick, then closes back near its open or beyond it. The Candle 2 shape.

You met the reversal shape in Lesson 5 and the expansion shape in Lesson 27. What's new is holding all three side by side and asking one question of each: how much range has already been used?

## The wick as a fuel gauge

TTrades' phrase for this is exactly right, so I'll borrow it: wick size is a fuel gauge for price movement.

> **Small wicks support further expansion in the same direction. Large wicks limit expansion potential because the range has already been exhausted.**

Think about what a wick is. It's distance price travelled and gave back inside one candle. A reversal candle with a long lower wick has already made a big run down and a big run back up. Two trips. Whatever energy that session had, a lot of it has been spent. A reversal candle with a small wick did the minimum: a poke below the level, a close back above. It's barely moved, and the tank is still full.

So the profile tells you the target:

| Candle 2 wick | What the next candle is likely to do | Sensible target |
|---|---|---|
| Small | Expand hard in the new direction | Previous candle's high or low, the nearest liquidity, then the standard deviation projections (Lesson 17) |
| Large | Retrace, not trend | The daily open, or the session high or low. Take it and go. |

This is the difference between a reversal that trends aggressively and one that just retraces, and it's decided by the wick, not by how convinced you are.

## When the T-Spot forms without a wick

The T-Spot (Lesson 16) is the zone where you expect the higher-timeframe wick to form. Two things can happen:

1. **The wick forms in the T-Spot.** Price dips into the zone, the lower-timeframe CISD confirms, price leaves. That's the setup, and the entry lives in that dip.
2. **The T-Spot forms without a wick.** Price opens near the T-Spot, or expands away before ever dipping into it. The candle is already running.

The second case tempts you to chase. Don't. TTrades' rule is: if the T-Spot forms without a wick, anticipate a retracement into it before expansion. A candle that hasn't made its wick yet usually still owes one. Wait for the dip, then look for the CISD. If the dip never comes and the candle just expands, you missed it, and that's fine. Missing a trade costs nothing.

## Timing inside the candle

Here's the part people skip. It isn't just the size of the wick; it's when it forms.

> **Expansion candles have small wicks created early into the candle.**

An hourly candle that makes its low in the first ten minutes and spends the next fifty expanding is an expansion candle. An hourly candle that grinds sideways for forty minutes and then dips is a different thing, and the dip late in the candle is more likely to be the start of the next candle's story than the wick of this one.

This gives you a filter for the inversion entries in Lesson 52: avoid inversions too late into the higher-timeframe candle. If the confirmation only arrives with a few minutes left, the candle you were trading is over, and you'd be entering on the strength of a candle that hasn't opened yet.

## Reading a candle while it's open

Put the three ideas together and you get a live checklist for any higher-timeframe candle:

1. Where did it open relative to the T-Spot?
2. Has it made its wick yet? Early or late?
3. How big is the wick relative to the range so far? Small keeps the expansion targets on; large means take the near target.
4. Is the body building in the direction of the bias?

If the answers are "in or above the T-Spot", "early", "small" and "yes", you're looking at an expansion candle forming. That's the candle the whole model exists to find.

## Key terms

- **Expansion candle**: small wicks, large body, momentum
- **Retracement candle**: a modest pullback candle with no displacement
- **Reversal candle**: a long opposing wick with a close back near the open
- **Fuel gauge**: the idea that wick size tells you how much range is left
- **Late wick**: a wick formed near the end of the candle, which usually belongs to the next candle's story

## Common mistakes

- **Expecting a trend from a big-wick reversal.** The range is spent. Take the near target.
- **Chasing a T-Spot that hasn't wicked.** Wait for the retracement into it.
- **Ignoring the clock.** A wick with ten minutes left in the candle isn't an early wick.
- **Reading the wick without the body.** A small wick with no body is a doji, not an expansion.
