## Tired of false breakouts?

Every lesson so far has ended by pointing here. Lesson 5 told you to "look for a change in the state of delivery." Lesson 6 told you to "find the state change." This is that tool — and it is the single most important idea in the course, because it is what converts a *shape* into *proof*.

## The definition

> **Change in State of Delivery (CISD) — a shift in momentum confirmed by a close beyond the opening of preceding candles.**

Or, stated for swing validation:

> **Price shifts direction by closing past the opening price of an opposing series of candles.**

Read that carefully, because the precision matters:

- Not the *high* or *low* of the opposing candles — the **opening price**.
- Not one candle — the **series** of candles that produced the move.
- Not a touch — a **close** beyond them.

### Why "delivery"

Think of price as being *delivered* in a direction. A run of down-close candles is bearish delivery. When price closes back above the opening of that entire series, the delivery has changed state — the mechanism that was pushing price down has been overturned, not merely paused.

That is the difference between a pullback and a reversal, made mechanical.

## Multi-timeframe approach

CISD is used top-down, with each timeframe doing a different job:

| Chart | Job |
|---|---|
| **Daily** | Identify a Daily Fair Value Gap — the major draw on liquidity |
| **Hourly** | Wait for a higher-timeframe CISD — confirmation |
| **2-Minute** | Execute on a lower-timeframe CISD — surgical entry |

The daily gives the destination. The hourly gives permission. The two-minute gives the price. Each step narrows risk without changing the idea.

## Standard deviation targets

Once a CISD confirms the reversal, you project targets by measuring the **explosive manipulation leg** — the swing that created the setup — and extending it:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **-1** | First target |
| **-2 to -2.5** | Primary projected target zone |
| **-4 to -4.5** | Maximum expansion |

The **-2 to -2.5 standard deviation** zone is the primary target. The **-4** extension is reserved for maximum-momentum conditions.

Targets gain confidence when they line up with something real:

| Target type | Confluence |
|---|---|
| Old highs / lows | Swept liquidity |
| -2 to -2.5 SD | Primary mathematical projection |
| -4 SD | Extended projection |

A projection that lands on a liquidity pool you already marked is far stronger than one that lands in empty space.

## The AMD entry model

CISD sits inside a repeating four-phase cycle:

| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Accumulation** | A tight consolidation range builds |
| **Manipulation** | An aggressive sweep out of that range |
| **Distribution** | CISD formation confirms momentum |
| **Entry** | Retest of the validated Order Block |

This is the skeleton beneath everything: the market builds a range, runs the stops on one side of it, genuinely shifts direction, and then delivers. The sweep you learned in Lesson 3 is *manipulation*. The Candle 2 closure from Lesson 5 is what manipulation looks like on one candle. The CISD is what proves distribution has begun.

### The trader's master checklist

1. **Accumulate** — identify the consolidation range
2. **Manipulate** — wait for the sweep out of it
3. **Confirm CISD** — the close beyond the opposing series
4. **Enter trade** — on the retest
5. **Take profits** — at the standard deviation targets

## Ready to wait for market confirmation?

The whole lesson is an argument for patience. A false breakout and a real reversal look identical *until the close*. CISD is the rule that makes you wait for the close — and waiting is what stops you being the liquidity in somebody else's setup.

## Key terms

- **CISD** — a close beyond the opening price of an opposing series of candles
- **Delivery** — the direction in which price is being moved
- **Manipulation leg** — the swing measured to project targets
- **Standard deviation (SD)** — a projection multiple of the manipulation leg
- **AMD** — Accumulation, Manipulation, Distribution
- **Order Block** — the entry zone left behind by the shift (Lesson 11)

## Common mistakes

- **Using the high/low of the opposing candles instead of the opening.** The definition is specific for a reason.
- **Accepting a wick through the level.** CISD requires a *close*.
- **Skipping the higher timeframe.** A 2-minute CISD with no daily reason behind it is noise at high resolution.
