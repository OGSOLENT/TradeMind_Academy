## Too many points is the problem

> **Use way too many points… and end up getting stopped out.**

By now you have a lot of levels available. Order blocks, fair value gaps, swing points, equilibrium, previous day high and low. Mark all of them on one chart and every candle touches something, which means everything looks like a setup and nothing is one.

This lesson fixes that with a **priority order**. Not a new tool. Just a rule for which of the tools you already have wins when several are in play.

## The point of reversal

First, what all these levels are for:

> **Point of Reversal — the protected swing.** The place price is expected to reset, or turn, and the anchor a trade is secured to.

A protected high or protected low is the anchor. Everything below is about finding the best *entry* into that turn.

## Priority one — Fair Value Gaps

> **Fair Value Gap (FVG) — the highest-priority gap between candles for a price reversal or continuation.**

If a valid FVG (Lesson 7a) sits in the range you are working with, that is your point of interest. Nothing else outranks it.

One warning attached to it:

> **Do not try to front-run it.**

Wait for price to actually reach the gap. Entering early because you expect it to get there is Lesson 14a's mistake in a different costume.

## Priority two — Swing Points

If there is no gap in the range, drop to the second option: a swing high or swing low.

> **Wait for a swing high or low to be swept out before trading.**

The swing is not the entry on its own. The *sweep* of it is. That is Lesson 3's liquidity logic and Lesson 14b's relevant-swing filter doing their job at execution level.

### What if an FVG is resting under a swing low?

This is the case that confuses people, and the answer is clean: the FVG still wins. It is priority one. A gap sitting beneath a swing low is where price is heading after it sweeps the swing, so the sweep is the manipulation and the gap is the destination.

## Priority three — CISD as last resort

> **CISD Retest Level — a last resort retest level when no gaps or swing points exist in the range.**

Two gates before you get here:

| Gate | Check |
|---|---|
| **Gate 1** | Is there a gap? If yes, use it. |
| **Gate 2** | Is there a swing point? If yes, use it. |
| **Final option** | Neither exists — use the CISD retest level. |

The CISD (Lesson 7) always confirms the shift. What is different here is using its *level* as the entry zone, which you only do when the range offers nothing better. It is a fail-safe, not a first choice.

The 50% principle applies here too: the midpoint of that CISD range is the reference.

## The mechanical trading checklist

The whole method in one line:

**FVG → Swings → CISD**

Work down the list. Take the first one that exists in your range. Do not mix them, and do not mark all three at once, which is exactly how you end up with too many points and a stop somewhere in the middle of them.

## The fractal model

The hierarchy is not a separate system. It slots into the fractal model (Module 7): the higher timeframe tells you *where* the reversal should happen, and this checklist tells you *which level inside that zone* to actually use.

## Key terms

- **Point of Interest (POI)** — a pre-marked level giving a reason for a trade
- **Point of reversal** — the protected swing the trade is anchored to
- **Priority one / two / last resort** — FVG, swing point, CISD retest
- **Front-running** — entering before price reaches the level
- **CISD retest level** — the fail-safe entry when the range has no gap and no swing

## Common mistakes

- **Marking every level you know.** The hierarchy exists to stop this.
- **Front-running the gap** rather than waiting for price to arrive.
- **Reaching for CISD first** because it is familiar. It is the last resort, by definition.
