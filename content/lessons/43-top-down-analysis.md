## The question this lesson answers

You have a lot of tools now. This lesson is about the order you use them in, because the order is what turns a pile of concepts into a decision.

## Three layers, three questions

> **Bias: which way? Narrative: where and when? Confirmation: now?**

| Layer | Timeframes | Question | Tools |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bias** | Weekly, daily | Which direction am I allowed to trade today? | Daily bias (Module 8), weekly profile (Module 13), IPDA ranges (Lesson 44) |
| **Narrative** | 4-hour, 1-hour | Where is price being drawn to, and roughly when? | Draw on liquidity (13), PD array stack (29), kill zones (36), market maker model (42) |
| **Confirmation** | 15-minute, 5-minute, 1-minute | Is the setup happening right now, at my level, in my window? | Sweep, shift, gap (40), CISD (7), turtle soup (41) |

Each layer answers one question and hands the answer down. The bias tells the narrative which pools of liquidity to care about. The narrative tells the confirmation which level and which hour to watch. The confirmation tells your finger when to move.

## The stack

Most people who trade this way settle on something close to:

- **Daily** for bias. One candle a day. Is it expanding, retracing, reversing?
- **4-hour** for the swing structure and the draw. Which old high or low is price reaching for this week?
- **15-minute** for the setup zone. Which gap or block, in which half of the range, in which kill zone?
- **5-minute or 1-minute** for the trigger. The sweep, the shift, the gap.

The numbers matter less than the principle. Some traders use weekly, daily, 1-hour, 5-minute. What never changes is the direction of reading: top down.

## Why never bottom up

Here's what bottom-up looks like: you open the 5-minute, you see a beautiful shift and a gap, you take it, and then you glance at the daily and notice you just bought into the middle of a daily sell-off. The 5-minute was telling the truth about the 5-minute. It just wasn't the timeframe that mattered.

A lower timeframe can only ever tell you *how* a move is happening. It can't tell you *whether* the move is the one worth trading. That comes from above. So the routine always starts at the top, and a setup that contradicts the layer above it is discarded, no matter how pretty.

## The routine

Run this before every session, in writing, in this order:

1. **Daily.** Bias for today: bullish, bearish, or no trade. One word.
2. **4-hour.** The draw: the specific high or low price is likely heading for. Mark it. Mark the range and equilibrium. Am I in premium or discount for my bias?
3. **1-hour and 15-minute.** The arrays in my half of the range, ranked. The kill zone I'll trade. The calendar: any high-impact news inside it?
4. **5-minute, in the window.** Wait for the sweep, the shift, the gap. Enter only when the confirmation happens at a marked array, inside the window, in the bias direction.
5. **After.** Journal it, which is Lesson 47.

If steps 1 to 3 don't produce a clear plan, step 4 doesn't happen. That's the whole discipline: the lower timeframe is only allowed to confirm a plan, never to invent one.

## When the layers disagree

They will. The daily is bullish, the 4-hour has just shifted bearish. Two honest choices:

- **Wait.** Let the 4-hour retracement finish, and buy when the 15-minute shifts back up in line with the daily. This is usually right.
- **Trade the 4-hour, small.** Treat it as a retracement trade against the higher bias, take the first target only, and size it as the counter-trend trade it is.

What you don't do is pretend the disagreement isn't there.

## Key terms

- **Bias, narrative, confirmation**: the three layers and their questions
- **Top down**: reading from the highest timeframe to the lowest, always
- **The routine**: the written pre-session pass through the layers

## Common mistakes

- **Starting on the entry chart.** It can only confirm, never decide.
- **Letting a pretty setup override the bias.** Discard it.
- **Skipping the writing.** The routine works because it's written, not remembered.
- **Trading a disagreement at full size.** Wait, or size it as counter-trend.
