## The question this lesson answers

Most of what you've learned is about *where*. The Silver Bullet is the model that's mostly about *when*. It's one hour, one fair value gap, and a small, realistic target, and it's the cleanest way to see time and price working together.

## The model in one line

> **Silver Bullet.** Inside a fixed one-hour window, enter on the first fair value gap that forms in the direction of the bias, and target the nearest liquidity.

There's no new array in it. The fair value gap is Lesson 7a. The bias is Module 8. The liquidity is Lesson 13. What's new is the frame: the hour.

## The three windows

| Window | New York time |
|---|---|
| London | 3am to 4am |
| **New York AM** | **10am to 11am** |
| New York PM | 2pm to 3pm |

The New York AM window is the one everyone means when they say Silver Bullet. It sits just after the 9:30 open has done its manipulation and just as the 9:50 macro has run, so the gap you're waiting for is very often already forming as the hour starts.

## The checklist

1. **Bias.** Know the day's direction before 10am. If you don't, you don't trade the window.
2. **Liquidity.** Mark the nearest pool in the bias direction: the previous day's high, a session high, equal highs. That's the target.
3. **The window opens.** From 10am, watch the 1-minute to 5-minute chart.
4. **The gap.** Wait for a displacement in the bias direction that leaves a fair value gap. Ideally it follows a small sweep against the bias, but the gap is the requirement.
5. **The entry.** A limit order in the gap, or a market entry on the return to it. Stop beyond the far side of the gap, or beyond the swing that made it.
6. **The target.** The liquidity from step 2. On indices this is often 5 to 15 handles. On forex, 15 to 20 pips. It's meant to be modest.
7. **The window closes.** If nothing has formed by 11am, there's no trade. Don't stretch the hour.

## Why it works

Three things line up in that hour:

- The manipulation of the open is usually finished, so the direction is cleaner.
- The 9:50 macro has typically created the gap.
- The London close window starts at 10am too, so there's real order flow, not lunchtime drift.

The modest target is the part beginners resist and the part that matters most. The model isn't trying to catch the day. It's trying to catch one clean leg with a tight stop, inside a window when that leg is likely. Take the leg, and let the rest of the day be someone else's.

## A worked example

Bullish daily bias. Previous day's high sits 12 handles above the 10am price. At 9:52 price runs a small low, then displaces up on the 5-minute, leaving a gap. At 10:04 price retraces into the gap. Entry there, stop 3 handles below the gap, target the previous day's high. Two to one on the risk, resolved by 10:40. That's a complete Silver Bullet.

## Variations you'll see

Some traders require the sweep before the gap, some don't. Some enter at the gap's 50%, the consequent encroachment, rather than its edge. Some trade all three windows, most only the AM. The core doesn't change: one hour, one gap in the bias direction, one nearby target.

## Key terms

- **Silver Bullet**: a one-hour window model built on a single fair value gap
- **The AM window**: 10am to 11am New York
- **The target**: the nearest liquidity in the bias direction, kept modest

## Common mistakes

- **Trading it without a bias.** No direction, no trade.
- **Stretching the hour.** 11am is the end.
- **Targeting the moon.** The model is a small leg with a tight stop.
- **Taking the first gap against the bias.** The gap has to point the way the day is going.
