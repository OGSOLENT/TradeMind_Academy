## The question this lesson answers

You already know how to validate a swing point with CISD and how protected swings define a trend. This lesson gives you the vocabulary the wider ICT world uses for the same ideas, and it adds one thing you don't have yet: a clean rule for telling a continuation break from a reversal break.

## Two kinds of break

Price moves in swings. In an uptrend it makes higher highs and higher lows. In a downtrend, lower highs and lower lows. Every time price trades through one of those swing points, you get a break. But not all breaks mean the same thing.

> **Break of Structure (BOS).** Price trades through a swing point in the direction of the current trend.

In an uptrend, a BOS is a new higher high. It tells you the trend is still doing what trends do. Nothing has changed, and you should keep trading with it.

> **Change of Character (CHoCH).** Price trades through a swing point against the current trend.

In an uptrend, a CHoCH is a break below the most recent higher low. That's the first crack. It doesn't prove a reversal, but it's the market failing to do what it was doing, and that failure is information.

| Break | Direction relative to trend | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| BOS | With the trend | Continuation. Carry on. |
| CHoCH | Against the trend | Warning. The trend may be finished. |

## From a break to a shift

A CHoCH on its own is weak. Price wicks through lows all the time. What makes it worth acting on is the way it breaks.

> **Market Structure Shift (MSS).** A CHoCH delivered with displacement, after a liquidity sweep.

Three ingredients, in order:

1. **A sweep.** Price runs above an old high (or below an old low) and takes the stops resting there. You met this in Lesson 3 as liquidity.
2. **The break.** Price then trades back through the opposite swing point, against the trend.
3. **Displacement.** The break happens on a big, decisive candle or series of candles that leave a fair value gap behind. Not a drift. A shove.

If you've only got the first two, be patient. If you've got all three, you've got a shift, and the fair value gap left by the displacement becomes the place to look for an entry. That's the whole 2022 model, which you'll meet properly in Lesson 40.

### Why displacement is the test

A gentle break can be absorbed. Price can trade through a low by a few ticks, find buyers, and carry on up. Displacement can't be absorbed so easily, because it shows that one side has been overwhelmed. The size of the move is the evidence.

This is the same idea as CISD from Lesson 7. CISD looks at the close of a run of candles. The MSS looks at the swing point and the speed of the break. They're two ways of asking one question: has delivery actually changed hands?

## Internal and external structure

Here's where most people get lost. Zoom out and a chart is a handful of big swings. Zoom in and every one of those swings is made of smaller swings, and those are made of smaller ones again. Which breaks count?

> **External structure is the swing structure of the timeframe you're trading. Internal structure is everything that happens inside one of its legs.**

An internal CHoCH on the 5-minute chart, inside a clean 1-hour uptrend, is usually just a retracement getting under way. It's not a reversal of the 1-hour trend. If you trade every internal break as a reversal, you'll fade every pullback and lose.

So the rule is:

- Decide direction on the higher timeframe (Module 6, Daily Bias, does this for you).
- Only treat an MSS on the lower timeframe as a reversal if it breaks *external* structure, or if the higher timeframe has already turned.
- Otherwise, an internal shift against the higher-timeframe trend is a retracement, and the trade is the continuation after it.

## Reading a shift step by step

1. Mark the trend on your bias timeframe: higher highs and higher lows, or the reverse.
2. Mark the most recent protected swing, the one that would have to break for the trend to be in doubt.
3. Wait for a run into liquidity beyond a recent extreme.
4. Watch the break of the protected swing. Ask two things: did it displace, and did it leave a gap?
5. If yes to both, mark the gap. That's your zone. If no, nothing has happened yet.

## Key terms

- **Break of Structure (BOS)**: a break with the trend
- **Change of Character (CHoCH)**: a break against the trend
- **Market Structure Shift (MSS)**: a CHoCH with displacement, after a sweep
- **Displacement**: a fast, decisive move that leaves an imbalance behind
- **External structure**: the swings of your trading timeframe
- **Internal structure**: the swings inside one of those legs

## Common mistakes

- **Calling every CHoCH a reversal.** Without displacement it's just a break.
- **Trading internal shifts against the higher timeframe.** That's fading a pullback.
- **Forgetting the sweep.** A shift that didn't take liquidity first has less behind it.
- **Marking the break instead of the gap.** The entry lives in the displacement's fair value gap, not at the swing point.
