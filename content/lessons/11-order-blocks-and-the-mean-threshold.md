## Ever been stopped out by a single tick?

That experience is what this lesson fixes. Being stopped out by one tick is almost never bad luck — it is an entry placed where everyone else placed theirs, with a stop where everyone else put theirs.

## The valid order block

> **Valid Order Block — price hits a key level, sweeps liquidity, and closes past the prior opening price.**

Three conditions, in order:

1. **Key level** — price reaches a higher-timeframe level (the POI requirement, again)
2. **Sweep** — it sweeps buy-side or sell-side liquidity there
3. **Displace** — it closes past the prior opening price (this is the CISD from Lesson 7)

The order block is the candle *from which* that displacement began. It is the footprint of the participants who caused the move, and price frequently returns to it before continuing.

Notice this is the same three-beat rhythm as everything else in Module 4: level → sweep → close. The order block is simply *where you enter* once that rhythm completes.

### Without a higher timeframe

> **Without a higher timeframe, it's just building equal highs to stop you.**

An order block with no higher-timeframe reason behind it is not an entry zone — it is a place where equal highs are forming, which is to say a liquidity pool, which is to say a trap. This is the fourth consecutive lesson making the same point, because it is the difference between a method and a habit.

## The mean threshold

Sometimes the displacement candle is enormous, and entering at its far edge would mean an impractical stop.

> **If the block is too large, use the 50% threshold for your entry.**

The **mean threshold** is the midpoint of the order block. Entering there instead of at the extreme:

- Halves the distance to your stop
- Keeps the invalidation logic intact
- Accepts a small chance of missing the entry entirely

This is the same equilibrium idea from Lesson 6, applied to a block instead of a candle range. Fifty percent recurs throughout this method: the EQ of a range, the EQ of a candle, the mean threshold of a block.

## Decoding wick sizes at the block

Wick reading (Lesson 4) determines whether to act:

| Shape | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Large body, small wick | **Expansion** — price is moving with intent |
| Small body, large wick | **Exhaustion / retreat** — the move is spent |

> **If we have a large opposing run... does it make sense price will rip down here? No.**

If the candle arriving at your order block has already burned its time and range forming a large wick, the expansion you are waiting for has no fuel left. Wait for the next candle.

## Stop-loss placement

Three rules:

- **Stop at the swing low** (or high) — the protected swing from Lesson 9
- **Watch for wicks** — expect price to probe; the stop belongs beyond the noise
- **Move the entry, not the stop**

That last rule is the important one. When a trade's risk feels too large, the instinct is to tighten the stop. That is backwards: the stop is at the invalidation point and moving it means you no longer know when you are wrong. Instead, **move the entry closer** — use the mean threshold, or drop a timeframe for a tighter entry. Risk shrinks; the invalidation stays honest.

## The perfect trade

The four-step sequence:

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **1. Setup** | Spot the high-timeframe level |
| **2. Confirm** | Wait for the block closure (close past the preceding opening price) |
| **3. Wait** | Wait for a small wick — a shallow opposing run |
| **4. Execute** | Execute at the swing low, stop beyond it |

Step 3 is the one most often skipped. It is Lesson 4's rule doing its job as a final filter: even with a valid order block at a valid level with valid confirmation, a large opposing run means *not yet*.

## Key terms

- **Order block** — the candle from which a displacement began; a return-to entry zone
- **Mean threshold** — the 50% midpoint of an order block, used for large blocks
- **Displacement** — the aggressive close past the prior opening price
- **Exhaustion** — a large-wick, small-body candle with no expansion left

## Common mistakes

- **Marking order blocks with no higher-timeframe reason.** You are marking a stop-hunt zone.
- **Tightening the stop instead of improving the entry.** The stop is the invalidation; it does not move for convenience.
- **Entering on a candle with a large opposing run.** Nothing left in the budget.
