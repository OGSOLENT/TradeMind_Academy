## Same chart, different account

Here's the thing to hold on to: everything in Modules 1 to 15 works identically on both. A Candle 2 closure on ES is a Candle 2 closure on US500. The T-Spot doesn't know what instrument you're using. What changes is the account behind the chart: who's on the other side, what it costs, what hours it keeps, what protects you, and what the tax office thinks. Those are the questions this lesson answers.

## The seven differences

| | Futures (Lesson 55) | CFDs and spread bets (Lesson 56) |
|---|---|---|
| **Counterparty** | The exchange's clearing house. One price for everyone. | Your broker. The broker's price, derived from the market. |
| **Regulation** | CFTC and NFA in the US; your broker's home regulator elsewhere | FCA, ESMA or ASIC rules for retail accounts. Not available to US retail. |
| **Leverage** | Set by exchange margin, and by broker intraday margin, which can be very high | Capped for retail: 20:1 on major indices, 30:1 on major FX |
| **Protections** | Can lose more than your deposit | Negative balance protection, 50% margin close-out |
| **Costs** | Commission plus exchange fees, visible per trade. Data subscription. No financing. | Spread on every trade, financing every night held, execution at the broker's discretion |
| **Hours** | 23 hours, Sunday evening to Friday evening, with a daily break | Often 24 hours, but spreads widen outside the cash session |
| **Tax (UK individual)** | Capital Gains Tax | CFDs: Capital Gains Tax. Spread bets: currently free of CGT. |

A few of these deserve a sentence.

**Price integrity** is the strongest argument for futures. The tick you see on ES was traded by someone. On a CFD the broker chooses the price, and while good brokers track the market closely, the incentive is theirs, not yours.

**Protections** are the strongest argument for CFDs. A retail CFD account cannot go negative and cannot run away from you. A futures account can do both, and the intraday margin makes it easier than it should be.

**Costs** cut both ways. For a day trader who's flat every night, futures are usually cheaper on the minis and roughly level on the micros. For anyone holding positions for days, the CFD financing charge is the deciding cost.

**Tax** is a UK-specific edge for spread betting that is real but should never be the reason you pick an instrument. A tax-free loss is still a loss.

## The decision guide

Start from your situation. Each line narrows the field.

**1. Where do you live?**
- United States: futures. CFDs aren't available to you.
- United Kingdom or Ireland: all three, and spread betting has the tax edge.
- European Union: CFDs at 20:1, or futures through a broker that gives you CME access.
- Elsewhere: check what your regulator allows retail clients and whether a broker you can verify offers it.

**2. What do you trade?**
- US index futures (ES, NQ), which is what this course draws: futures are the native instrument, and the micros make them affordable.
- Currencies or gold: CFDs are the retail route in most countries; futures exist but the contracts are large.
- Individual shares: CFDs or the shares themselves; single-stock futures are impractical for a beginner.

**3. When do you trade?**
- New York session, cash hours: either. Futures have the volume; CFD spreads are tight.
- London or Asia on a US index: the future is open and liquid; CFD spreads widen. Futures.
- Holding overnight or for days: futures, because there's no financing charge. Or accept the cost knowingly.

**4. How much can you fund, and what's the cost of a mistake?**
- Under about £500 to $500: a micro CFD or spread bet at pennies per point is the only size that lets you make beginner mistakes cheaply. A prop-firm evaluation (Lesson 58) is the other route.
- £1,000 to $2,000 and up: MES and MNQ give you the real market at $1.25 and $0.50 a tick with a sensible cushion.

**5. How much do you value protection over price?**
- If the words "negative balance protection" made you relax, that's information. Start on a retail CFD account.
- If "the price everyone sees" is what you want, that's information too. Futures.

## The honest summary

Most people learning this material end up on **CME index futures via the micros**, because the course is drawn on them, the price is real, and the costs are visible. A UK trader with a small account and a preference for protection can make a good case for a **spread-betting account on US500 or US100**, and lose nothing in the chart. Both are fine. The mistake is picking by leverage, or by an advert.

## What to do first, either way

1. **Demo for a month** on the instrument you chose, with the sizing rules from Module 3 and the journal from Lesson 47. Learn the platform's order types until entering a bracket order is boring.
2. **Trade the smallest size that exists** for another month. One micro, or pennies per point. The point is to feel real fills and real slippage without real consequences.
3. **Only then** decide whether to size up, and only if the journal says your execution grades are A.

Nobody has ever lost money in step one, and most of what goes wrong later is skipping it.

## Key terms

- **Price integrity**: whether the price you trade was made by a central market or by your broker
- **Financing charge**: the nightly cost of holding a CFD, absent on futures
- **Retail protections**: the leverage caps, close-out rule and negative balance protection on CFD accounts
- **Micro contract**: one tenth of an E-mini; the affordable way onto the real market

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing by leverage.** Higher leverage is not an advantage; it's a faster way to be wrong.
- **Choosing by tax.** A tax-free loss is a loss.
- **Holding CFDs for weeks without pricing the financing.** Check the daily charge before you decide to hold.
- **Skipping the demo month.** The platform will surprise you. Let it do so for free.
