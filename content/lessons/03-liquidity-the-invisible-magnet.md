## The question this lesson answers

Lesson 2 left you able to read a candle's story but not to say *where* price is likely to go next. Liquidity is the answer. It is the single concept that turns candle reading into a directional framework.

## What liquidity is

Behind every chart is an order book full of resting orders — stop losses, pending entries, and unfilled interest. Large participants need volume to fill positions. They cannot buy in size where nobody is selling.

So price is drawn toward the places where orders are clustered.

> **Liquidity is the fuel price moves toward, not away from.**

Once you internalise this, the movement stops looking arbitrary. Price is not wandering; it is travelling from one pool of orders to the next.

## Buyside and sellside liquidity

There are two kinds, and they sit in predictable places.

**Buyside liquidity**
Buy stop orders resting **above old highs**. Anyone short has a stop above the high; anyone waiting to buy a breakout has an order there too. That cluster is targeted by larger participants who need buy orders to pair against their sells.

**Sellside liquidity**
Sell stop orders resting **below old lows**, acting as a magnet pulling price down. Anyone long has a stop below the low.

The uncomfortable implication, and the point of the whole lesson:

> **Your stop loss is somebody else's liquidity.**

That is not a conspiracy — it is mechanics. Orders cluster in obvious places because most people mark the same obvious levels.

## Where to find liquidity

A repeatable, three-step method for marking a swing point:

1. **Locate the point** — find a specific high or low on the chart.
2. **Check the left side** — is there a lower high (or higher low) to the left of it?
3. **Check the right side** — is there a lower high (or higher low) to the right of it?

A high with lower highs on *both* sides is a genuine swing high, and the buyside liquidity sitting above it is real. The same logic inverted gives you a swing low and its sellside liquidity.

### The levels worth marking

- **Previous day high and low** — the most-watched intraday levels
- **Previous week high and low** — the weekly equivalents
- **Equal highs and equal lows** — two or more touches at the same price stack orders in one spot, making them especially strong magnets
- **Session highs and lows** — Asian, London and New York each leave their own

Mark these before the session starts. This map is what turns reaction into preparation.

## Putting it together

With liquidity mapped, every candle you learned to read in Module 1 gains a purpose. The question is no longer "what is this candle?" but:

> **Will price expand toward the target, or reverse entirely?**

Expansion means price is travelling toward a liquidity pool. Reversal means price has *reached* one, taken the orders, and turned. The candle shape tells you which — and Lesson 4 shows how the wick size decides it.

A practical sequence for a reversal:

1. **Let it close.** Let the large reversal candle completely close. Do not try to catch a falling knife.
2. **Observe the open** of the next candle.
3. **Wait for a small wick** — a shallow opposing run (Lesson 4 defines this).
4. **Enter** on the confirmed expansion continuation.

## Key terms

- **Liquidity** — clusters of resting orders that attract price
- **Buyside liquidity** — buy stops above old highs
- **Sellside liquidity** — sell stops below old lows
- **Equal highs / equal lows** — repeated touches at one price, stacking orders
- **Liquidity pool** — a concentration of resting orders at a level
- **Sweep** — price running through a level to take the orders resting there

## Common mistakes

- **Marking every high and low.** Only swing points with lower highs (or higher lows) on both sides carry meaningful liquidity.
- **Assuming a sweep means reversal.** Taking liquidity is a *reason* for a turn, not proof of one. Proof comes in Module 4.
- **Trading breakouts of obvious equal highs** without asking who is buying from you at that price.
