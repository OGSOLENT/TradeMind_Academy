## The question this lesson answers

Module 8 taught you to set a daily bias and to let the wick form. This lesson explains *why* the wick forms, and it gives the day a shape you can plan around.

## One candle, three jobs

Take a bullish daily candle. It opens, dips below the open, then rallies and closes near the high. That dip is the wick you were told to wait for. Power of Three names the parts.

> **Accumulation, then Manipulation, then Distribution. The open, the false move, then the true one.**

| Phase | On a bullish day | When it tends to happen |
|---|---|---|
| **Accumulation** | Price trades sideways around the open, building positions | Around the midnight open, through Asia |
| **Manipulation** | Price drops below the open, running the sell stops and drawing in shorts | London open, or the New York open |
| **Distribution** | Price rallies through the range and expands to the high, delivering the day's real move | New York session |

On a bearish day, flip it: quiet around the open, a rally above the open that traps buyers, then the sell-off.

The daily candle's wick is the manipulation. Its body is the distribution. When you look at a finished daily candle you're looking at the three phases stacked into one shape.

## Why the false move comes first

Think about what the day needs. To rally, the market needs sellers to buy from, and it needs the stops of early shorts as fuel. The cheapest way to get both is to push price down first: shorts pile in, longs get stopped out below the open, and the algorithm has a pool of resting orders to expand through.

That's manipulation. It isn't personal, and it isn't random. It's the run of liquidity from Lesson 13, happening on schedule.

## Using it

Power of Three gives you a plan for a bullish day:

1. **Don't buy at the open.** Accumulation is a range, and the manipulation hasn't happened yet.
2. **Wait for the drop below the open.** Ideally it sweeps a clean low, the previous day's low or the Asian range low.
3. **Look for the shift.** A Market Structure Shift back above the open, with displacement, is the sign manipulation is done.
4. **Enter in the discount.** The retracement into the gap left by the shift, below the open, is the buy.
5. **Target distribution.** The day's draw on liquidity from Lesson 13, above.

The open is the reference the whole model turns on. Bullish day: you want to buy *below* it. Bearish day: sell *above* it. If you're buying above the open on a bullish day you're buying in premium, and Lesson 28 told you what happens there.

## It's fractal

Power of Three isn't only a daily thing. Every candle on every timeframe has an open, a false move and a true move. A weekly candle accumulates on Monday, manipulates into Tuesday or Wednesday and distributes into the end of the week, which is exactly the shape of the weekly profiles in Module 13. A 4-hour candle does it in miniature. Once you see it at one scale you'll see it everywhere, and that's the point.

## Key terms

- **Accumulation**: the range around the open where positions are built
- **Manipulation**: the false move against the day's direction that runs stops
- **Distribution**: the true move, the expansion to the day's target
- **The open**: the reference; buy below it on bullish days, sell above it on bearish days

## Common mistakes

- **Buying the open on a bullish day.** The manipulation hasn't happened.
- **Reading the manipulation as the bias.** The first move is the fake one.
- **Expecting the phases to be equal in size.** Accumulation is small, distribution is the big one.
- **Only applying it to the daily.** It's every candle.
