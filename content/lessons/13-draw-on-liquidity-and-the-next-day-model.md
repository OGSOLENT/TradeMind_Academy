## How do pros predict markets before the open?

They don't predict. They identify a **draw**.

> **It doesn't require some magic crystal ball.**

If you can name the liquidity pool price is being pulled toward, you have your daily bias — not as a forecast, but as a direction with a specific destination and a specific invalidation.

## Draw on Liquidity (DOL)

> **Draw on Liquidity (DOL) — the price level or liquidity pool the market is actively pulled toward, like a giant magnet.**

This is Lesson 3's liquidity concept promoted into a bias tool. Instead of "there is liquidity above and below," you commit to *which* pool is the active target. That commitment is your bias.

Once you have named the DOL:
- Your **direction** is toward it
- Your **target** is it
- Your **invalidation** is the structure that says the market chose the other pool

## Previous day highs and lows

The primary intraday draws remain PDH and PDL. Price gravitates toward one of them, and the daily bias is a decision about which.

## Weekly ranges

Zoom out one level and the previous week's high and low generate the draw for the coming week:

| Event | Draw on Liquidity |
|---|---|
| Reach Previous Week High and **close below** | Target the Previous Week Low |
| Sweep Previous Week Low and **reverse** | Target the Previous Week High |
| **Inside bar** (no extreme taken) | Trend to the nearest weekly extreme |

The logic is the same in all three rows: when one side's liquidity is taken and rejected, the *opposite* pool becomes the draw. When neither is taken, the nearest one is.

## Swing points revisited

The mechanism behind those rows, stated precisely:

> **Price reaches beyond an old swing point but fails to close outside its range, pulling price back in.**

This is the Candle 2 closure from Lesson 5 — reappearing at weekly scale. The failure to close outside is what turns a sweep into a reversal, and what redirects the draw to the opposite side.

## The Next Day Model

Three steps for anticipating tomorrow:

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1. Take old swing** | Price takes out an old swing high or low |
| **2. Fail & reject** | Price fails to displace and closes back inside |
| **3. Anticipate** | Expect an **OLHC** (bullish) or **OHLC** (bearish) candle |

The third step is the useful part, and it needs unpacking.

### OLHC and OHLC

These describe the *order in which* a candle's four points are made:

- **OLHC** — Open, then **Low**, then High, then Close. Price opens, dips to make the low early, then rallies and closes high. **A bullish day**: the low forms early, leaving the rest of the period for expansion upward.
- **OHLC** — Open, then **High**, then Low, then Close. Price opens, pushes up to make the high early, then falls and closes low. **A bearish day**.

This is Lesson 4's opposing run at daily scale. In a bullish OLHC day the shallow opposing run is the early dip — and if you know to expect it, you are waiting to buy that dip rather than being scared out by it.

## Trading London and New York

The two sessions that matter are London and New York. Session timing is a filter on when the model is likely to trigger — and it is the "kill zone" idea that the execution checklist assumes.

> *A dedicated kill zones / session timing lesson is a recommended addition; it is referenced here but never taught.*

## The execution checklist

Five boxes before entering:

1. **Kill Zone** — are we in a session window that matters?
2. **Fair Value Gap** — is there a valid POI?
3. **Stop** — is the invalidation defined? (the protected swing, Lesson 9)
4. **Draw on Liquidity** — what is the target?
5. **Risk to Reward** — does the maths justify the trade?

The example given: risking **1 unit** to make **2.55 units**. Names the risk and the reward in the same breath — which is the only way item five can be answered honestly.

> *A dedicated risk management and position sizing lesson is a recommended addition; "risk 1 unit" is used here without being taught.*

## Are your stops fuelling someone else's expansion?

The closing question ties the module together. If your stop sits at the obvious place, it is part of a liquidity pool — and that pool may be the very DOL that draws price. Knowing where liquidity sits lets you avoid donating to it.

## Key terms

- **DOL (Draw on Liquidity)** — the pool price is actively being pulled toward
- **Inside bar** — a period that takes neither the prior high nor the prior low
- **OLHC** — Open-Low-High-Close: a bullish daily sequence
- **OHLC** — Open-High-Low-Close: a bearish daily sequence
- **Kill zone** — a session window in which setups are expected

## Common mistakes

- **Having a direction without a destination.** "Bullish" is not a bias; "bullish, drawing to the previous week high" is.
- **Ignoring the early opposing run** in an OLHC day and being shaken out of a correct idea.
- **Skipping item five.** A valid setup with poor risk-to-reward is still a bad trade.
