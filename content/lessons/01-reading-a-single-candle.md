## The question this lesson answers

A chart looks like random noise until you can read one candle. Every candle is a small, complete story about a fight between buyers and sellers over a fixed period of time. Learn to read one, and the chart stops being noise.

## OHLC — the foundation

Every candle, on every timeframe, records exactly four prices:

| Point | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Open** | The first traded price of the period |
| **High** | The furthest price reached upward |
| **Low** | The furthest price reached downward |
| **Close** | The last traded price of the period |

The **body** is the distance between open and close. The **wick** is everything beyond it — the ground price covered but did not hold.

The close matters most. A high that price could not hold is a rejection; a close is an agreement.

## The three candle types

Everything on a chart is one of three shapes. You identify them by comparing **body size to wick size**.

| Type | Wick | Body | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Directional** | Small | Large | Price confidently moved one way for most of the period |
| **Reversal** | Large | Small | Price aggressively rejected a direction and reversed course |
| **Indecision** | Long, both sides | Tiny | A tug-of-war; open and close are nearly identical |

**Directional candle.** Buyers (or sellers) took control early and held it. Little was given back. This is expansion — the market moving with intent.

**Reversal candle.** Price pushed one way, then was violently rejected. The long wick is the evidence of that rejection. The story: *someone was trapped.*

**Indecision candle.** Neither side won. Long wicks on both sides with a tiny body mean the period ended roughly where it started. Often a pause before the next real move.

### Worked example — a bearish reversal candle

1. **Open** — the candle opens.
2. **Push high** — buyers take control, pushing price up.
3. **Reversal down** — sellers aggressively push price back down.
4. **Close** — price closes *below* the open.

The result is a candle with a long upper wick and a red body. The upper wick is the record of the buyers who were overwhelmed.

## Timeframes and context

Here is the idea that changes everything:

> **Every timeframe that has a candle has a lower timeframe making that candle.**

One daily candle is built from 96 fifteen-minute candles. A single daily candle with a long lower wick is not one event — it is an entire lower-timeframe downtrend, a bottom, and a reversal, compressed into one shape.

This means a candle is never the whole story; it is a *summary* of a story happening one level down. Zooming out gives you context. Zooming in gives you detail. You need both.

The three sessions that build the day — **Asian**, **London**, and **New York** — each contribute a different part of that story.

## The Rule of Three

A simple, testable pattern for anticipating a shift:

- Look for **three consecutive days of expansion** in one direction.
- Expect a **shift in market momentum**.
- Anticipate a **reversal or indecision candle** next.

Markets rarely expand in a straight line indefinitely. After a third strong directional day, the probability of a pause or a turn rises. This is not a trade signal on its own — it is a prompt to start paying attention.

## Key terms

- **OHLC** — Open, High, Low, Close: the four data points of a candle
- **Body** — the distance between open and close
- **Wick** — price covered beyond the body but not held
- **Expansion** — a directional move with intent (large body, small wicks)
- **Rejection** — price reaching a level and being pushed away (a large wick)

## Common mistakes

- **Reading colour instead of shape.** A green candle with a huge upper wick is not bullish. Body-to-wick ratio tells you more than colour.
- **Ignoring the close.** The high of a candle is where price *failed*. The close is where price *agreed*.
- **Treating one candle as a signal.** A candle is context, not an instruction.
