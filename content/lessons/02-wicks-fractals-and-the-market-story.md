## Random chaos, or a specific story?

To an untrained eye a chart is scribble. To a trained one it is a sequence of clues, each candle a footprint left by the participants who moved price. This lesson turns the four sections of Lesson 1 into a way of *reading* rather than *labelling*.

## What a wick really is

In Lesson 1 you learned a wick means rejection. Now the precise version:

> **A wick on a daily chart is simply an aggressive reversal that happened on a lower timeframe.**

Put a daily candle with a long lower wick side by side with the 15-minute chart that built it, and you will see the wick is not a single event. It is a complete lower-timeframe move: a decline, a bottom, and a sharp reversal — all compressed into one line on the daily.

This reframes the wick from a shape into a **place where something happened**. Something you can go and look at.

### The session story inside one candle

A classic daily candle, decomposed:

1. **Asian session — accumulation.** The session chops sideways in a tight range, building positions.
2. **New York open — displacement.** Price aggressively displaces lower, taking out the range.
3. **Reversal into the close.** Price violently reverses upward, closing near the high.

On the daily chart that is one green candle with a long lower wick. On the 15-minute chart it is three distinct phases with an obvious turning point.

## The fractal ecosystem

Two rules capture the structure of every market chart:

- **15-minute candles build the Daily candle.**
- **Wicks are lower-timeframe trends.**

This is what "fractal" means here: the same shapes repeat at every scale. A reversal on the 15-minute chart looks like a reversal on the daily chart, just smaller. Once you can read one candle, you can read every timeframe — the vocabulary does not change.

The practical consequence: **when a daily candle is forming, you can watch its story unfold live on a lower timeframe** instead of waiting for the daily close to tell you what happened.

## Reading a multi-day sequence

Candles read in sequence tell a longer story. A common four-day sequence:

| Day | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Day 1** | Directional expansion |
| **Day 2** | Continued expansion |
| **Day 3** | Third expansion day |
| **Day 4** | Anticipate a reversal or indecision candle |

This is the Rule of Three from Lesson 1, drawn out over a week. Days 1–3 are the market moving with intent. Day 4 is where you shift from *following* to *watching*, because three days of expansion have usually delivered the move that was intended.

The skill being trained here is **anticipation, not prediction**. You are not forecasting day four. You are recognising a state where a change becomes more likely, and preparing to see it if it comes.

## Key terms

- **Fractal** — the same structures repeating across all timeframes
- **Displacement** — an aggressive, one-sided move away from a level
- **Accumulation** — a sideways, range-bound phase where positions build
- **Expansion sequence** — consecutive directional days in the same direction

## Common mistakes

- **Treating a wick as decoration.** The wick is the most information-dense part of the candle. It marks where the lower-timeframe reversal happened.
- **Analysing only one timeframe.** The daily tells you *what* happened; the 15-minute tells you *how and when*.
- **Forcing a story onto noise.** Not every candle is meaningful. The sequences worth reading are the ones at levels that matter — which is Module 2.
