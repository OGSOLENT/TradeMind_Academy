## Trading at the wrong times

Every checklist since Lesson 13 has opened with "Kill Zone" and never explained it. This is that lesson.

The problem it solves: most of the trading day is **choppy price action**, and most of the range is delivered in a few concentrated windows. Trading the quiet hours means paying spread and commission for noise.

> **Killzones — specific time windows with higher volatility for catching larger ranges.**

Time is a filter, exactly like the point-of-interest hierarchy is a filter. A perfect setup at the wrong hour is usually not a setup.

## All times are New York time

Before any table makes sense:

> **All times shown are in New York time, or Eastern Standard Time.**

Because of daylight savings, a window pinned to London or your local clock drifts twice a year relative to the New York session, which is where the volume actually is. Set your charts to New York time and leave them there.

## Session windows

The windows differ by instrument, which is the detail most people miss:

| Session | Forex | Indices |
|---|---|---|
| **Asia** | 20:00 – 00:00 | 20:00 – 00:00 |
| **London** | 02:00 – 05:00 | 02:00 – 05:00 |
| **New York AM** | opening bell onward | from 09:30, key move often ~10:00 |
| **New York PM** | — | from ~13:30 |

Forex and indices share the Asia and London windows. They separate in New York, because index futures key off the 09:30 cash open while forex does not.

Each session also gets a colour in the video's scheme — London green, NY AM orange, NY PM yellow — which is simply a way to see at a glance which window a candle belongs to.

## The daily cycle — connecting the sessions

The sessions are not independent. They form a sequence:

| Time (EST) | What happens |
|---|---|
| **20:00** | **Asia** begins and sets the stage — builds the range |
| **02:00** | **London** interacts with Asia liquidity — the breakout |
| **08:30 – 09:30** | **New York AM** opens and reacts — often the reversal |

Read as one story: **Asian range → London breakout → New York reversal → consolidation.**

This is the AMD cycle from Lesson 7 laid over the clock. Asia accumulates, London manipulates by running the Asian range, and New York distributes in the true direction.

### The textbook sequence

A clean bullish day looks like:

1. **Sweep the Asia low** — the manipulation
2. **Displace back into the range** — the shift
3. **Create an FVG** on that displacement — the point of interest (Lesson 7a)
4. **Target the Asia high** — the draw on liquidity (Lesson 13)

Every element there is something you have already learned. The kill zone tells you *when* to expect it.

## The Silver Bullet

> **Silver Bullet — a 1-hour window to find opportunities drawing to a specific liquidity pool.**

Rather than watching a three-hour session, you narrow to a single hour and take only setups drawing toward one identified pool. It is the tightest version of the same discipline: fewer windows, fewer levels, fewer trades.

## Setting it up on a chart

The video walks through configuring a kill zone indicator. The sequence:

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **1. Add indicator** | An ICT kill zone / pivots indicator |
| **2. Set timezone** | Ensure the chart is on New York time |
| **3. Remove labels** | Turn off default text to declutter |
| **4. Input times** | Set the precise hours for your instrument |

Step 2 is the one that silently breaks everything if skipped. Step 4 matters because, as the table above shows, the correct hours depend on whether you trade forex or indices.

## Key terms

- **Kill zone** — a defined window of higher volatility
- **EST / New York time** — the reference clock for every window
- **Daily cycle** — Asian range → London breakout → New York reversal
- **Silver Bullet** — a one-hour window targeting a specific liquidity pool
- **Session colour coding** — a visual aid for identifying the active window

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving charts on local time.** Every window drifts, twice a year.
- **Using forex windows on indices.** New York differs; the others do not.
- **Trading outside the windows** because a setup "looks good". Time is part of the setup.
