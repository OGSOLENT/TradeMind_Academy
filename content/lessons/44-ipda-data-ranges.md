## The question this lesson answers

Module 8 sets bias from the daily candle. But the daily candle lives inside a bigger range, and that range decides which of your daily biases turn into weeks of trend and which fizzle. This lesson is how to see that range.

## The claim

> **IPDA.** The Interbank Price Delivery Algorithm. The idea that price is delivered on a schedule to reference points measured over the last 20, 40 and 60 trading days.

You don't have to accept the mechanism. What's useful is the practice: three rolling ranges, each with a high, a low and an equilibrium, that give you a higher-timeframe version of premium and discount and a set of targets that refresh every day.

## The three ranges

Count back from today in trading days and mark the highest high and lowest low of each window:

| Range | Lookback | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| **20-day** | The last four weeks | The intraday and short-swing draw. Most day traders use this one. |
| **40-day** | The last eight weeks | Intermediate targets and the current regime: are we in premium or discount for the medium term? |
| **60-day** | The last twelve weeks, roughly a quarter | The major draw, and the level where quarterly reversals tend to happen |

For each, draw the 50%. Above it is premium at that scale, below it is discount, exactly as in Lesson 28 but measured over weeks instead of one leg.

## How to read them

Two questions, every week:

1. **Where am I in the 40-day range?** If price is in the upper half, the medium-term context favours selling premium and the daily bullish biases are fighting the tide. If it's in the lower half, bullish biases have the wind behind them.
2. **Which unfilled reference is nearest?** An old high or low from the 20-day range that hasn't been taken yet is a draw. Unfilled gaps inside the range are draws. The nearest one in the bias direction is a target, and the one on the other side is the sweep to expect first.

The 60-day range gets checked less often. Its extremes are where the quarter tends to turn: a new 60-day high, taken and rejected, is often the start of a reversal that runs for weeks.

## The quarterly rhythm

There's a seasonality to the idea. The 60-day window is roughly a quarter, and the claim is that the algorithm re-anchors around the turn of each quarter: late March, late June, late September, late December. Around those turns, expect the 60-day reference points to be revisited and the medium-term direction to be reconsidered. You don't need to trade the turn. You need to know it's coming, because a daily bias set the week before a quarterly shift is worth less.

## Putting it under the daily bias

The bias routine from Lesson 43 gets one more line at the top:

0. **IPDA.** Where is price in the 40-day range, and what's the nearest unfilled 20-day reference in each direction?

If the daily bias points the same way as the 40-day context, trade it with confidence. If it points against, trade smaller and take the first target, because you're trading a retracement of the bigger range.

## Key terms

- **IPDA**: the Interbank Price Delivery Algorithm, the scheduled-delivery idea
- **20 / 40 / 60-day ranges**: rolling lookbacks with a high, a low and an equilibrium each
- **Reference point**: an unfilled high, low or gap inside a range
- **Quarterly shift**: the re-anchoring around the turn of each quarter

## Common mistakes

- **Redrawing the ranges by eye.** Count trading days, or use an indicator that does.
- **Trading the 60-day like an intraday level.** It's context and a quarterly target.
- **Ignoring the 40-day premium or discount.** It decides which daily biases have the wind.
- **Treating the algorithm claim as the point.** The practice is what matters.
