## The pairs

The model runs on pairs: a higher timeframe where you count candles (Lesson 49) and read profiles (Lesson 50), and a lower timeframe where the CISD confirms the swing and the entry lives. The pairs are chosen so that the lower timeframe has enough candles inside one higher-timeframe candle to form a swing and a CISD, but not so many that the noise swallows the structure.

| Higher timeframe (count and profile) | Lower timeframe (CISD and entry) | Candles inside |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | Daily | about 21 |
| Weekly | 4-hour | 30 |
| Daily | 1-hour | 23 (futures) |
| 4-hour | 15-minute | 16 |
| 1-hour | 5-minute | 12 |
| 15-minute | 1-minute | 15 |

Twelve to thirty lower-timeframe candles per higher-timeframe candle. That's the sweet spot. Pair the daily with the 5-minute and you have 276 candles to read; you'll find a CISD in every one of them and none of them will mean anything. Pair the hourly with the 30-minute and you have two candles, which can't form a swing at all.

The pair TTrades locks the Pro indicator to is the **1-hour with the 5-minute**, and that's the pair I'd learn first. The hourly candle is big enough to carry a real wick and a real expansion, and the 5-minute gives you a dozen candles to confirm it in.

## The one-timeframe-up rule

Bias doesn't come from the pair you're trading. It comes from the pair above it.

> **Auto Bias references the fractal model one timeframe above your selected pair.** Trading the hourly and 5-minute, the bias comes from the 4-hour and 15-minute model.

So the full stack for the hourly model is three layers, not two:

1. **4-hour and 15-minute**: which way. Is the 4-hour making a Candle 2, 3 or 4, and which direction does it point?
2. **1-hour and 5-minute**: the setup. Count the hourly candles inside that 4-hour story, find the T-Spot, wait for the 5-minute CISD.
3. **1-minute** (Lesson 52): the entry refinement, if you want it.

This is the same top-down logic as Lesson 43, with the model's own vocabulary. The higher pair gives the bias; the lower pair gives the trade. When the 4-hour says up and the hourly is forming a Candle 2 low inside it, the hourly setup is with the bias, and that's the one to take. When the hourly is forming a Candle 2 *against* the 4-hour direction, it's a retracement of the 4-hour, and you either skip it or treat it as a scalp to the near target.

## Sessions and pairs

Not every pair suits every session. Volume and range change through the day, and the pair should match them.

| Session (ET) | Character | Pairs that fit |
|---|---|---|
| Asia, 19:00 to 00:00 | Slow, ranging | 1-hour or 30-minute confirmation. Fewer, slower setups. |
| London, 02:00 to 05:00 | The first real expansion | 1-hour, 30-minute or 15-minute confirmation |
| New York, 08:30 to 11:00 | The most volume | 15-minute for continuations, 5-minute for reversals |

The New York line is worth reading twice. Continuations (a Candle 4 story, joining a move that's already confirmed) can be confirmed on the 15-minute, because the direction isn't in doubt and you don't need a fine-grained turn. Reversals (a Candle 2 story, catching a turn) want the 5-minute, because the turn is a small event and a bigger candle would blur it.

If you've done Lessons 34 to 39 you'll recognise this as the session anatomy with the model laid on top. The Silver Bullet window at 10:00 is a place where the hourly and 5-minute pair earns its keep.

## Pick one pair

The mistake I see most is switching pairs mid-session. You lose the trade on the hourly, drop to the 15-minute and 1-minute to find something, and take a setup that isn't in the model at all.

Decide before the session:

- Which pair am I trading today? (Start with 1-hour and 5-minute.)
- Which pair gives my bias? (One up: 4-hour and 15-minute.)
- Which session am I trading, and does the pair fit it?

Then stay there. If there's no setup on your pair, there's no setup. The hourly candle will still be there tomorrow.

## A worked alignment

Say the 4-hour chart printed a Candle 2 low at 02:00 and is now expanding: bias up. It's 09:30 in New York. On the hourly, the 09:00 candle opened in the upper half of the 08:00 candle and is dipping. That's the hourly's turn to be a Candle 4 with the 4-hour bias behind it. You drop to the 5-minute. At 09:45 price sweeps the 09:00 open, and at 09:55 a 5-minute candle closes above the opening range high: CISD, protected low formed. That's the setup. Entry on the retest, stop under the protected low, target the projections from the 4-hour's Candle 2.

Three layers, one direction, one pair traded. That's the whole alignment.

## Key terms

- **Timeframe pair**: a higher timeframe for the count and a lower one for the confirmation
- **One-timeframe-up rule**: bias comes from the pair above the one you trade
- **Candles inside**: how many lower-timeframe candles fit in one higher-timeframe candle; 12 to 30 is the working range
- **Session fit**: matching the pair to the session's volume and range

## Common mistakes

- **Pairing timeframes too far apart.** Hundreds of candles inside means hundreds of false CISDs.
- **Taking bias from the pair you trade.** Bias is one up.
- **Confirming reversals on the 15-minute in New York.** Reversals want the 5-minute; the 15-minute is for continuations.
- **Switching pairs when the first one doesn't deliver.** That's how a plan becomes a hunt.
