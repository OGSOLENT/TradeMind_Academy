## The model

> **Liquidity sweep, then Market Structure Shift with displacement, then entry on the fair value gap the displacement left, inside a kill zone, in the direction of the higher-timeframe bias.**

That's the whole thing. Three steps and two conditions.

| Step | What it is | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Sweep | Price runs an old high or low and takes the stops | 3, 13 |
| 2. Shift | A CHoCH with displacement back the other way | 26 |
| 3. Gap | Entry on the retracement into the fair value gap from the shift | 7a, 28 |
| Condition: bias | The trade goes with the daily direction | Module 8 |
| Condition: time | It happens in a kill zone | 36 |

## Step by step, bullish

1. **Bias.** The daily is bullish (Module 8). You are only looking for longs.
2. **Draw.** Mark the sellside liquidity beneath price: the previous day's low, the Asian low, equal lows. One of those is where the sweep should happen.
3. **Wait for the kill zone.** London or New York AM.
4. **The sweep.** Price trades below one of the marked lows. Ideally on a wick, ideally quickly. That's manipulation.
5. **The shift.** Price reverses and breaks the most recent lower-timeframe swing high with displacement, leaving a fair value gap. On the 1-minute to 5-minute chart, this is a candle or two you can't miss.
6. **The entry.** A limit at the gap, usually its 50%, or a market order when price returns to it. Stop below the sweep low.
7. **The target.** The buyside liquidity above: the previous day's high, the session high, equal highs. The first target is the nearest; the second is the day's draw on liquidity.

Bearish is the mirror: sweep of a high, shift down through a swing low, sell in the bearish gap, stop above the sweep high, target the lows.

## Timeframes

- **Bias:** daily, sometimes 4-hour.
- **Draw and levels:** 1-hour and 15-minute.
- **Sweep, shift and entry:** 5-minute, 3-minute or 1-minute.

The lower you go for the entry, the tighter the stop and the more false shifts you'll see. Most people find the 5-minute is the honest compromise. The 1-minute is for after you've got the model working on the 5.

## What makes it good

Every step has a reason the previous step feeds into. The sweep gives the move fuel and traps the wrong side. The shift proves the trap sprang. The gap is where the trapped side exits and the winning side reloads. The bias makes sure you're on the side the higher timeframe is delivering for. The kill zone makes sure the algorithm is actually running.

Take any one of the five away and you've got a much weaker trade. That's a useful test: if you can't name the sweep, the shift, the gap, the bias and the window, you don't have a 2022 model, you have a hunch.

## What it isn't

It isn't a guarantee. Sweeps fail, shifts fail, gaps get closed through. That's why the stop lives below the sweep low and why Module 3 sized the position for it. Run it enough times with the rules kept and the edge shows up. Run it loosely and it's indistinguishable from guessing.

## Key terms

- **The 2022 model**: sweep, shift, gap, with bias and time
- **Sweep**: the run of liquidity that starts it
- **Shift**: the displacement break that confirms it
- **Gap**: the fair value gap the entry lives in

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping the sweep.** A shift without a sweep is a weaker trade.
- **Entering at the shift.** The entry is the retracement into the gap.
- **Running it against the daily bias.** It's a continuation model, not a reversal one.
- **Running it at lunch.** No window, no model.
