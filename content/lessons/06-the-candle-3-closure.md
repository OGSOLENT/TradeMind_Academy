## Real reversal, or just a trap?

Lesson 5 gave you Candle 2. But price does not always turn on schedule. Sometimes the sweep happens and the reversal comes one candle late. That delayed version is the **Candle 3 closure** — and knowing both keeps you from either missing the trade or forcing one that is not there.

## Two kinds of closure

First, a distinction that underpins everything:

**Continuation closure**
Price takes out a previous high or low and **closes completely beyond it**. The level did not hold. The move continues.

**Reversal closure**
Price sweeps a previous high or low but **closes back inside the range**. The level held. The move failed.

Same sweep, opposite meaning — decided entirely by where the candle closes. This is why the close matters more than the extreme.

## Candle 2 vs Candle 3

| | Candle 2 | Candle 3 |
|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Immediate reversal | Delayed reversal |
| **Action** | Sweeps the previous high or low | Engulfs Candle 2's body |

**Candle 2** is the immediate reversal: it sweeps and closes back in, all in one period.

**Candle 3** is the delayed reversal: Candle 2 swept the level but *failed to close back inside*. Candle 3 then closes through and **engulfs Candle 2's body**, completing the rejection one period late.

The rule for which to use:

> **If Candle 2 lacks a closure, wait for print.**

Do not assume the setup is dead because Candle 2 did not deliver. And do not pre-empt Candle 3 by guessing — wait for it to actually print.

## Context still decides

Just as in Lesson 5:

> **Is a perfect closure always a valid trade?**

No.

> **I'm not just going to choose random points; you need both of those** — the closure *and* a reason (a key level, a fair value gap, a swept liquidity pool).

A textbook Candle 3 engulfing closure in the middle of a range is not a trade. The same closure at a level you marked yesterday is.

## Framing the setup

Once you have a valid Candle 3 closure, the entry is mechanical:

1. **Wait** for a valid Candle 3 closure.
2. **Mark the range** — the *entire* range of Candle 3, high to low.
3. **Target the equilibrium (EQ)** — the 50% midpoint of that range — for the entry.

You are not entering at the close. You are waiting for price to retrace back into the middle of the candle that just proved the reversal. That midpoint is the equilibrium, and it gives a far better entry price with a tighter, more logical stop.

### Advanced tactics

- **Drop to a lower timeframe** for a more precise entry inside the EQ zone.
- **Find the state change** on that lower timeframe (Lesson 7) as confirmation.
- **Stop on the protected swing** — place the stop beyond the swing the setup created, not at an arbitrary distance (Lesson 9 defines protected swings).

## The non-negotiable rules

The four conditions, all required:

1. **Needs a key level.** No POI, no trade.
2. **C2: immediate reversal.** Candle 2 sweeps and closes back in — *or*, failing that:
3. **C3: engulfing body.** Candle 3 closes through and engulfs Candle 2's body.
4. **Enter at equilibrium.** The 50% of the closure candle's range.

## True reversals or random noise?

The closing question of the lesson is the discipline it is trying to build. Random swing points produce shapes that *look* like reversals constantly. A true reversal has all four conditions: level, closure, engulf, and an equilibrium entry. Everything else is a pattern you found because you were looking for one.

## Key terms

- **Continuation closure** — closes completely beyond the swept level; the move continues
- **Reversal closure** — closes back inside the range; the move failed
- **Engulfing body** — Candle 3's body fully covering Candle 2's body
- **Equilibrium (EQ)** — the 50% midpoint of a range, used as the entry
- **Protected swing** — the swing the setup creates, used for stop placement (Lesson 9)

## Common mistakes

- **Abandoning the setup when Candle 2 fails to close back in.** That is exactly the condition for waiting for Candle 3.
- **Entering at the close of Candle 3** instead of waiting for the retrace to equilibrium.
- **Pattern trading without a level.** Repeated for the third lesson running because it is the mistake that costs the most.
