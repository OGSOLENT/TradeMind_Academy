## Is a reversal a trap, or a trend?

You can now confirm a single turn (Lesson 7). This lesson scales that up: a series of confirmed turns is a **trend**, and the thing that holds a trend together is the **protected swing**.

## The protected swing

> **Protected swing — a high or low in the market expected to remain intact as the current trend continues.**

In an uptrend, each pullback creates a low. If the trend is genuine, that low should not be revisited — the market has moved on. That low is *protected*.

Protected swings are the skeleton of a trend. A true trend continuation is a sequence of them, each one higher than the last. The moment one breaks, the trend structure it belonged to is finished.

## How protected swings form

There are exactly two origins, and both end the same way:

| Formation type | Origin point | Confirmation |
|---|---|---|
| **Sweep-based** | Price sweeps a high or low | Close through the opposing series |
| **Fair Value Gap** | Price reaches into an FVG | Close through the opposing series |

Note the confirmation column: **identical**. Whether the swing began with a liquidity sweep or with a retracement into a fair value gap, it is validated the same way — a CISD.

> *A full lesson on Fair Value Gaps is a recommended addition; the current videos use FVGs as a point of interest without defining them.*

### The three-step formation

For a bullish protected swing low:

1. **Sweep low** — price sweeps out a short-term low.
2. **Identify** — find the down-close candles that made that low.
3. **Close above** — wait for price to close above those down-close candles.

Once that close happens, the low is protected and the swing is valid.

## Wicks and expansions — the shape of a trend

Trend structure and candle mechanics are the same thing at different scales.

> **Expansion candle — a candle with small wicks and a large body, making a shallow opposing run early.**

This is Lesson 4's rule restated in trend language. A healthy trend is built from expansion candles: each leg forms its extreme early, gives back little, and closes decisively. When candles start producing large wicks and small bodies, the trend is losing the energy that sustains it.

## Targets and invalidations

This is the practical payoff, and the reason protected swings matter more than any pattern.

> **I'm using it as my invalidation... to give me good risk-to-reward.**

The protected swing converts:

**An abstract pattern → a hard, mathematical invalidation point.**

Your stop is not a guess, a round number, or a fixed distance. It is a specific price that, if traded through, proves your read was wrong. That is what makes risk-to-reward calculable rather than hopeful:

- **Risk** = entry to the protected swing
- **Reward** = entry to the target (Lesson 7's standard deviations, or a liquidity pool)

If the trade invalidates, it invalidates for a *reason you defined in advance*. That is the whole point.

### Executing the strategy

1. Identify the protected swing that the setup created.
2. Place the stop beyond it — not at an arbitrary distance.
3. Measure risk from entry to that swing.
4. Take the trade only if the reward justifies the risk.
5. If the protected swing breaks, the structure is invalid. Exit; do not rationalise.

## Key terms

- **Protected swing** — a high or low expected to hold while the trend continues
- **Sweep-based formation** — a protected swing originating from a liquidity sweep
- **FVG-based formation** — a protected swing originating from a retracement into a fair value gap
- **Invalidation** — the price that proves the idea wrong
- **Expansion candle** — small wicks, large body, shallow early opposing run

## Common mistakes

- **Calling every pullback low a protected swing.** It is only protected once a close through the opposing series confirms it.
- **Moving the stop past the protected swing** to avoid being stopped out. That deletes the only objective invalidation you had.
- **Ignoring deteriorating candle shape.** Growing wicks and shrinking bodies are the trend telling you it is tiring.
