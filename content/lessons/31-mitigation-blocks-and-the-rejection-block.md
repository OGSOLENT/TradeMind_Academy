## The question this lesson answers

Last lesson, a failed order block with a sweep became a breaker. This lesson deals with the failed block that didn't have a sweep first, and with the wick that gets rejected at a swing point. Together with the breaker, that's the whole block family.

## The mitigation block

> **Mitigation block.** A failed order block with no liquidity sweep before the failure. Price comes back to it later to "mitigate" the orders left behind, and it acts as continuation in the direction of the original move.

The word mitigation is banking language. When a position is left at a loss, coming back to the price it was opened at, to close it flat, is mitigating it. The idea is that the orders which started the original move got stranded when the block failed, and the return to the block is where they get cleared.

The sequence for a bullish mitigation block:

1. Price makes a swing low and rallies.
2. On the pullback it forms a bearish order block (an up candle before a drop), and price drops through it.
3. Crucially, price does **not** take out the old swing low. It stops short. No sweep.
4. Price turns and rallies through the block again.
5. Later, price returns to that block from above. It acts as support and the rally continues.

Compare that to the breaker: same failed block, but in the breaker's case the old low was swept before the reversal. The sweep is the only structural difference, and it changes the block's job completely.

| | Breaker block | Mitigation block |
|---|---|---|
| Sweep before the failure? | Yes | No |
| Job | Reversal, or the start of a new leg | Continuation of the existing leg |
| Strength | Higher, because trapped traders are behind it | Lower, because there was no trap |
| Best used | After a Market Structure Shift | Inside an established trend |

The practical upshot: if the trend is running and you missed the entry, the mitigation block is often the second chance. If the trend has just turned, look for the breaker.

## The rejection block

The third member is easy to miss because it's only a wick.

> **Rejection block.** The wick above a swing high (or below a swing low) where price was refused. The block is the span from the body's edge to the tip of the wick.

Think about what a long wick at a swing high means. Price went up there, found no willingness to trade at those prices, and was pushed back. Those prices were rejected. When price returns, the same rejection tends to repeat, so the wick's span becomes a zone.

The rejection block is usually the *first* array a retracement touches, because it sits right at the extreme. In the matrix from Lesson 29 it ranks near the top for that reason. It's small, it's precise, and it's the one people don't mark because it isn't a body.

## How to mark them

| Block | Zone to mark |
|---|---|
| Order block | The body of the last opposing candle |
| Breaker | The body of the failed block |
| Mitigation | The body of the failed block |
| Rejection | From the top of the body to the tip of the wick (for a high) |

For all four, the body is the tighter first choice. Widen to the wick only if the body keeps getting missed by a few ticks on your instrument.

## Choosing between them

You'll rarely get one on its own. A typical retracement in a bullish trend might hit a rejection block, then an order block, then a mitigation block from an earlier leg. Use the matrix ranking, use time, and use the lower-timeframe confirmation. The array that gets the confirmation is the one that counts. The others were just on the way.

## Key terms

- **Mitigation block**: a failed order block with no prior sweep, used for continuation
- **Mitigate**: to return to an open price and clear stranded orders
- **Rejection block**: the refused wick beyond a swing point
- **The block family**: order, breaker, mitigation and rejection blocks

## Common mistakes

- **Trading a mitigation block as a reversal.** It's a continuation array.
- **Confusing it with a breaker.** Check for the sweep. That's the only difference.
- **Ignoring wicks at swing points.** The rejection block is often the first level to react.
- **Marking wicks on every block.** Start with bodies.
