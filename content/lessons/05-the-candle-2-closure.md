## The pattern

> **A lot of times a Candle 2 closure will mark a turning point in the market.**

**Candle 2 Closure** — *price sweeps a previous extreme but closes back inside the previous candle's range.*

Two things must both happen:

1. Price **sweeps** beyond the prior candle's high or low (taking the liquidity resting there — Lesson 3).
2. Price **closes back inside** that prior candle's range (failing to hold the new extreme).

The sweep grabs the orders. The close-back-in proves the move failed. Together they are the signature of a trap: participants who bought the breakout or were stopped out at the extreme are now offside, and their exits fuel the move the other way.

| Scenario | Sweep | Closure | Expected next move |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bullish** | Sweeps the low | Closes above | Upward |
| **Bearish** | Sweeps the high | Closes below | Downward |

## The four-candle sequence

The Candle 2 closure is one beat in a longer rhythm:

| Candle | Role |
|---|---|
| **Candle 1** | The reference range — the candle whose high/low will be swept |
| **Candle 2** | The sweep and the reversal closure |
| **Candle 3** | The initial continuation |
| **Candle 4** | Follow-through expansion |

Naming candles by position rather than by shape is what makes this mechanical. Candle 2 is *always* the one that sweeps Candle 1 and closes back inside. This numbering returns in every later module — the daily bias lessons, the fractal model, and all four weekly profiles.

## The point of interest — why most of these fail

Here is the part that separates this from pattern-spotting:

> **Does every reversal closure guarantee a market turn?**

No. And the video is blunt about it:

> **Looking for every pattern that has a sweep and a closure back in is unlikely to work.** You need an actual reason.

The distinction is **context vs shape**. The shape (sweep + close back in) will appear dozens of times across a chart, most of them meaningless. What makes one worth trading is *where* it happens — at a **Point of Interest (POI)**: a level you had already marked before price arrived, such as a swept liquidity pool, a previous day's high or low, an order block, or a fair value gap.

No POI, no trade. The pattern is the trigger; the POI is the reason.

## Spotting them on charts

A three-step routine:

1. **Find swings.** Mark any place the market made a turn.
2. **Check closure.** Did it sweep an extreme and close back inside?
3. **Confirm POI.** Does it align with a valid Point of Interest?

Two out of three is not a setup.

## Confirming on a lower timeframe

Once the Daily Candle 2 closure is identified at a POI, you do not enter blind:

> **Confirm this on a lower timeframe... look for a change in the state of delivery.**

That phrase — change in state of delivery — is the mechanical proof that the turn is real. It gets its full definition in Lesson 7. For now: drop a timeframe and look for evidence that price has genuinely shifted direction, not just paused.

## The fractal timeframe pairs

The model works at every scale. Pair a bias timeframe with an execution timeframe:

| Bias (HTF) | Execution (LTF) |
|---|---|
| Monthly | Daily |
| Daily | Hourly |
| 4-Hour | 15-Minute |
| Hourly | 5-Minute |

### The top-down workflow

1. Spot a **Daily Candle 2 closure at a POI**.
2. **Anticipate the bias** for the next day.
3. **Drop down to the Hourly chart.**
4. **Wait for a Change in State of Delivery.**
5. **Look for an Hourly Candle 2 Closure** to execute.

The same pattern, at two scales, in agreement. The daily gives permission; the hourly gives the entry.

## Key terms

- **Candle 2 closure** — sweeps a previous extreme, closes back inside the prior range
- **Point of Interest (POI)** — a pre-marked level giving a reason for the reversal
- **Reference range** — Candle 1, whose extreme gets swept
- **HTF / LTF** — higher timeframe (bias) and lower timeframe (execution)
- **Change in State of Delivery (CISD)** — mechanical proof of a directional shift (Lesson 7)

## Common mistakes

- **Trading the shape without the context.** The single most common failure. A sweep-and-close-back-in in the middle of nowhere is noise.
- **Entering on the daily closure itself** without dropping down for confirmation.
- **Renumbering the candles to fit.** Candle 2 is the one that sweeps Candle 1 — not whichever candle you would like it to be.
