## The drill

You are looking at a bearish swing point: price sweeps above a level, then turns down. It looks like a valid short.

**But how do you tell a genuine swing point from a fakeout?**

## The hidden danger

The trap is inside the wick.

When price sweeps above a swing point and reverses, the reversal *looks* complete on the higher timeframe. But that wick contains an entire lower-timeframe move — and you have no idea what happened inside it until you look.

That unexamined wick is the hidden danger. It is where a fakeout hides in plain sight.

## The steps

1. **Identify the sweep.** Price reaches above the swing point.
2. **Go inside the wick.** Drop to a lower timeframe and open up the price action that formed it.
3. **Look for a sharp Change in State of Delivery.** Inside that wick, did price close decisively through the opening of the opposing candle series?
4. **Decide.**
   - **CISD present** → the swing point is **VALIDATED**. Trade it.
   - **No CISD** → there is no proof. It is a fakeout. Skip it.

## The rule

> **To prove it's a real swing point, there must be a sharp change in the state of delivery inside the wick. If there is no CISD, the swing point is not validated.**

## Why this drill matters

Lesson 2 taught you that a wick is a lower-timeframe trend. Lesson 7 taught you how to prove a directional shift. This drill is the two ideas colliding into a single, repeatable habit:

**Never trust a wick you have not looked inside.**

Do this on ten historical swing points on your own chart. You will find that a meaningful share of textbook-looking reversals contain no CISD at all — and those are precisely the ones that would have stopped you out.

---

## Practice task

Open any chart on the daily timeframe. Find five swing highs where price swept a prior high and closed lower.

For each one:

1. Drop to the 15-minute chart and locate the wick.
2. Mark the series of up-close candles that made the high.
3. Ask: did price *close* below the **opening** of that series?
4. Label each as VALIDATED or FAKEOUT.

Then check what price did over the following days. Compare your validated set against your fakeout set.

## Check your understanding

1. Where exactly do you look for the CISD when validating a swing point?
2. What do you do when you drop into the wick and find no CISD?
3. Why is a higher-timeframe reversal candle, on its own, not proof of a swing point?
