## The missing definition

Every lesson from Module 4 onwards has used the fair value gap as a Point of Interest — the place a setup is allowed to happen. This lesson defines it.

## Defining an FVG

> **Fair Value Gap (FVG) — a three-candle pattern where candle 1 and candle 3 highs and lows do not overlap.**

Look at any three consecutive candles:

- **Valid FVG:** candles 1 and 3 **do not** overlap, leaving a clear space between them.
- **Not an FVG:** candles 1 and 3 **overlap**, closing the space completely.

The gap is the untraded space between candle 1's extreme and candle 3's extreme, created by candle 2's aggressive move straight through the middle.

### Why the gap matters

> **One side is offered while the other is not, creating a market inefficiency.**

In a normal, balanced market both buyers and sellers transact across every price. In the gap, price moved so fast that one side never got filled. That imbalance is an inefficiency — and markets tend to return to inefficiencies to rebalance them, which is exactly why an FVG works as a magnet and a Point of Interest.

## SIBI and BISI — translating the jargon

Two labels for the same structure, named by which side was left unfilled:

| Term | Full name | Forms during | Acts as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BISI** | Buyside Imbalance, Sellside Inefficiency | An aggressive move **up** | Support on a return |
| **SIBI** | Sellside Imbalance, Buyside Inefficiency | An aggressive move **down** | Resistance on a return |

Strip the acronym and it is simply: *which direction created the gap?* A gap made by a violent rally is a bullish gap, and price returning to it is expected to find buyers.

## The 50 percent rule

A gap is not all-or-nothing. Price interacts with it in stages:

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Start** | Price touches the beginning of the gap |
| **50% mark** | Price reaches **consequent encroachment** — the midpoint |
| **Complete fill** | Price pushes entirely through |

**Consequent encroachment** is the name for the 50% level of the gap, and it is the level that matters. Equilibrium again — the same midpoint logic you met in Lesson 6 for candle ranges and Lesson 12 for the daily range. Fifty percent recurs throughout this method because it is where a range is fairly priced.

### Invalidation

> **An FVG is invalidated if a candle body closes over the 50% line or outside the gap.**

Note the precision, consistent with everything in Module 4: a **body close**, not a wick. Price may spike through the midpoint and recover — that is not invalidation. A candle that *closes* beyond it is.

## FVG inversion — when the rules fail

The most useful part of the lesson, because it turns a failure into information:

> **FVG Inversion — an invalidated gap flips its function to act as future support or resistance.**

| Before | After inversion |
|---|---|
| Old **SIBI** (bearish gap) | Now acts as **support** for price moving higher |
| Old **BISI** (bullish gap) | Now acts as **resistance** for price moving lower |

A gap that failed to hold has told you something real: the side that created it has been overwhelmed. The level does not become meaningless — it changes sides. This is the same principle as the Golden Fractal Rule in Lesson 15: a valid structure that fails is itself a signal.

> **Inversions don't have to be immediate; an old gap can be tested much later.**

Mark inverted gaps and leave them on the chart. They stay relevant.

## Comparing market gaps

Not every space on a chart is an FVG:

| Feature | FVG | Volume imbalance | Standard gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Body gap?** | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **Wick overlap?** | No | Yes | No |
| **Trading occurred?** | Some | Some | **None** |

- A **volume imbalance** has bodies apart but **wicks overlapping** — price did trade through, just thinly.
- A **standard gap** (a weekend or news gap) has *no trading at all* in the space — the market was closed.
- An **FVG** sits between the two: fast one-sided delivery, not an absence of market.

Getting this distinction right stops you marking weekend gaps as setups.

## Key terms

- **Fair Value Gap (FVG)** — three candles where 1 and 3 do not overlap
- **BISI / SIBI** — bullish / bearish imbalance labels
- **Consequent encroachment** — the 50% midpoint of the gap
- **Invalidation** — a body close beyond the 50% line or outside the gap
- **FVG inversion** — an invalidated gap flipping to the opposite function
- **Volume imbalance** — bodies apart, wicks overlapping

## Common mistakes

- **Marking gaps where candles 1 and 3 overlap.** That is not an FVG.
- **Treating a wick through the midpoint as invalidation.** It takes a body close.
- **Discarding a failed gap.** An invalidated gap inverts — it becomes a level on the other side.
- **Confusing weekend gaps with FVGs.** No trading occurred in a standard gap at all.
