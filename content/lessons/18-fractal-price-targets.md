## Are you leaving money on the table by exiting early?

Lesson 17 gave you mathematical targets. This lesson gives you **structural** ones — levels the market itself has already drawn — and shows how to hold for them without giving back the trade.

## The fractal target

> **Fractal target — a target derived from a higher timeframe's previous candles to find swing highs or lows.**

Rather than projecting a multiple of your entry leg, you look *up* a timeframe and ask: what has the market not yet reached? Those untaken swing highs and lows are the destinations, because the liquidity resting there (Lesson 3) is the draw.

## Identifying targets

Three steps:

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **1. Switch time** | Switch to a higher-timeframe reference |
| **2. Find levels** | Identify previous **untaken** highs or lows |
| **3. Mark targets** | Plot those levels as your actual targets |

### The key target rules

1. **Focus on swing points.** Real swings (Lesson 3's left-side/right-side test), not every minor extreme.
2. **Ignore taken levels.** A level price has already traded through has had its liquidity consumed. It is spent — it is no longer a magnet.
3. **Consolidations are ok.** Choppy, ranging price between you and the target does not invalidate it. Price does not need a clean path.

Rule 2 does most of the work. Traders repeatedly target levels that were swept days earlier and wonder why price never reaches them. The orders are gone.

## Aligning timeframes

Entry and target timeframes pair, exactly as bias and execution paired in Lesson 12:

| Entry | Target |
|---|---|
| **5-Min** | Hourly |
| **Hourly** | Daily |
| **5-Min → Hourly** | Weekly |

The nesting is deliberate — Russian dolls. A 5-minute entry aims at an hourly objective. Hold longer and the same trade can run to a weekly objective, but only if the higher-timeframe structure supports it.

This pairing prevents both errors at once: taking a 5-minute entry and expecting a weekly move, or taking an hourly entry and closing it at a 5-minute target.

## The SMT exception

A neat piece of practical nuance for correlated markets:

- **Gold**: the target low is **NOT** hit.
- **Silver**: the target low **IS** hit.

> **When a correlated asset takes out the low, the target has been hit.**

If your objective is a liquidity pool and the *correlated* market has taken its equivalent pool, the liquidity has been consumed even though your chart shows the level untouched. Waiting for your chart to print the exact price risks watching the reversal happen without you.

This is SMT logic used for **exits** rather than entries — Lesson 20 covers the entry side.

## Max risk to reward

How to hold for large targets without surrendering profits:

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **1. Enter trade** | Enter on a lower timeframe |
| **2. Take profit** | Take partial profit at the short-term target |
| **3. Hold runners** | Hold the remainder for higher-timeframe targets |

> **Take off a portion at a short-term target and hold a runner.**

The lower-timeframe entry gives a small stop, which makes the risk-to-reward large when measured against a higher-timeframe target. Taking partials at the near target banks a result and — importantly — removes the pressure that causes people to close a runner early. The remainder can then reach for the fractal target with, as the video puts it, **zero pressure**.

## Key terms

- **Fractal target** — a higher-timeframe untaken swing high or low
- **Untaken level** — a level price has not yet traded through; still holds liquidity
- **Runner** — the remaining position held for a higher-timeframe target
- **Partial profit** — closing part of a position at a nearer target
- **SMT exception** — a correlated asset taking the equivalent level counts as the target being hit

## Common mistakes

- **Targeting levels already swept.** The liquidity is gone.
- **Mismatching entry and target timeframes.** A 5-minute entry does not justify a weekly target on its own.
- **Closing runners early out of anxiety.** The partial exists precisely to remove that anxiety.
