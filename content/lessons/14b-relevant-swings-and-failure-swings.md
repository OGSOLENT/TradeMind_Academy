## Not every swing is worth trading

Lesson 3 taught you to *find* swing points, and Lesson 8 to *validate* one with CISD. This lesson adds the filter in between: of all the swings on the chart, which are solid enough to build on?

## Relevant swings — solid ground

> **Relevant Swing — a swing point with valid separation from previous highs or lows.**

The operative words are **valid separation**. A swing that stands clear of the levels around it is *solid ground*: price had to travel a real distance to make it, so the liquidity resting beyond it is meaningful.

| | Reads as |
|---|---|
| **Wide, valid separation** | A true reversal point |
| **Closely stacked** | Bait |

## Failure swings — the trap

> **Failure Swing — stacked highs or lows lacking valid separation, acting as liquidity traps.**

When several highs cluster within a few points of each other, they are not a level — they are a pool. Price grinding out a series of near-identical highs is building the very liquidity it intends to run. Treating one of those as a swing point puts your stop inside the pool.

This is Lesson 3's "equal highs are a stronger magnet" seen from the other side: strong magnet, terrible swing point. The same feature that makes a level attractive to price makes it dangerous to trade *from*.

The practical filter: before marking a swing, ask whether it stands clearly apart from what surrounds it. If you have to squint to separate it from three neighbours, it is a failure swing.

## Confirming the daily wick

The rule the module keeps returning to:

> **Let the candle form its daily wick before trading the expansion.**

The addition here is *where in the session* to expect it.

### The New York open timeline

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| **9:00 AM** | Opposing move or manipulation — often a **failure swing**, a trap |
| **9:30 AM** | Stock market open; volatility spikes; the **true move** begins |
| **10:00 AM** | Hourly candle closure — the **relevant swing point**, and the prime window for entry |

Read together with the two definitions above, this is the lesson's core insight: the swing formed at 9:00 is usually the trap, and the one confirmed by the 10:00 hourly close is usually the tradeable one. Same shape, different hour, opposite meaning.

Waiting for the 10:00 closure costs you the first thirty minutes and saves you the trap.

## Session volatility — adapting to speed

The confirmation timeframe should match how fast the session moves:

| Session | Volatility | Closure | CISD |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Asia** | Low / slow | 4-Hour | 4H → 1H |
| **London** | Medium pace | 4-Hour | 15m / 30m |
| **New York** | High / fast | 1-Hour | 5m / 15m |

Stated as a rule of thumb:

- **Slower market** → wait for **4-hour closures**.
- **Violently fast market** → use **5-minute shifts**.

A slow Asian session produces 5-minute noise that means nothing; a fast New York session resolves before a 4-hour candle ever closes. Matching resolution to pace is what stops you being simultaneously too twitchy and too late.

## Why your bias matters — the ultimate reality

The module's closing argument, and it echoes Lesson 14:

> **You can mechanically confirm the wick high or low… and still be wrong.**

Confirmation tells you a swing is structurally valid. It does not tell you the higher-timeframe draw was read correctly. When bias and confirmation disagree, bias wins.

### SMT as the tie-breaker

For ambiguous extremes, compare correlated assets (Lesson 20):

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **Two assets** | Watch two correlated markets, e.g. ES and NQ |
| **Observe sweep** | See if one sweeps a low while the other fails to |
| **Trade away** | Use the divergence to confirm the extreme, and execute |

## The confluence checklist

The whole lesson in three lines:

1. **Find a relevant swing** — valid separation, solid ground
2. **Avoid failure swings** — stacked levels are bait
3. **Wait for the wick** — let the daily wick form before trading the expansion

## Key terms

- **Relevant swing** — a swing with valid separation from surrounding highs/lows
- **Valid separation** — clear distance that makes a swing structurally meaningful
- **Failure swing** — stacked highs/lows with no separation; a liquidity trap
- **Solid ground** — the quality a relevant swing provides for building a trade
- **Prime window** — the post-open hourly closure where entries are taken

## Common mistakes

- **Marking every swing on the chart.** Most lack separation and are traps.
- **Trading the 9:00 move.** That is usually the manipulation, not the day.
- **Using one confirmation timeframe in every session.** Asia and New York run at different speeds.
- **Assuming confirmation beats bias.** It does not.
