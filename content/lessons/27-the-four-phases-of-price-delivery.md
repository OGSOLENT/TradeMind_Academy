## The question this lesson answers

Every setup in this course is a bet on what happens next. That bet is a lot easier when you can say, plainly, what price is doing right now. It turns out there are only four answers.

## The four phases

> **At any moment price is expanding, retracing, reversing or consolidating. There's no fifth option.**

| Phase | What it looks like | What it usually means |
|---|---|---|
| **Expansion** | Large-bodied candles in one direction, small wicks, gaps left behind | The market has chosen a side and is delivering price quickly |
| **Retracement** | Price gives some of the expansion back, slowly, with overlapping candles | The expansion is resting before it continues |
| **Reversal** | Price gives it all back and then some, with a shift in structure | Delivery has changed hands |
| **Consolidation** | Price chops sideways in a range with no follow-through either way | The market is building the energy (and the liquidity) for the next expansion |

The reason this matters: the same candle means different things in different phases. A bearish candle inside a bullish expansion is a pullback and probably a buying opportunity. The same candle after a reversal is the trend.

## Expansion

Expansion is what everything else exists for. It's the leg you actually get paid on. You recognise it by big bodies, little wicks and the fair value gaps it leaves behind, the ones from Lesson 7a.

Expansion has one rule that saves you money: **don't chase it.** By the time expansion is obvious, the good entry was behind you. Expansion is what you're trying to be in, not what you try to catch.

## Retracement

After an expansion, price rests. It drifts back into the leg it just made, usually into a fair value gap or an order block, on overlapping candles with no conviction. This is where entries live. A retracement into the discount of a bullish expansion, with the higher-timeframe bias still bullish, is the core buy in this whole method.

The trick is telling a retracement from a reversal while it's happening. Two tells:

- A retracement respects the imbalance. It comes back to the gap and stops. A reversal closes through it.
- A retracement doesn't break the protected swing from Lesson 9. A reversal does, with displacement.

## Reversal

A reversal is an expansion in the opposite direction, and it starts with the Market Structure Shift from Lesson 26. If you've spotted the sweep, the break and the displacement, you're watching phase three begin.

Reversals are where beginners lose the most, for a simple reason: they try to call them early. The reversal is only confirmed after the shift. Before that, it's a retracement until proven otherwise.

## Consolidation

Consolidation is price going nowhere. Equal highs, equal lows, a range. It feels like nothing is happening, but two things are:

1. Liquidity is building above and below the range. Every stop placed just outside the box is fuel for the next expansion.
2. The market is waiting for time. Consolidation very often ends at a session open or a macro window, which Module 9 covers.

The rule for consolidation is to do nothing inside it. Trade the expansion out of it, after the sweep of one side.

## The sequence

The phases follow each other in a loop that's more reliable than it has any right to be:

> **Consolidation, then expansion, then retracement, then either more expansion or a reversal, then consolidation again.**

Once you can name where you are in that loop, you know roughly what's coming and, just as usefully, what isn't. Nobody gets a reversal straight out of a consolidation without an expansion first.

## Naming the phase, quickly

Look at the last five to ten candles on your timeframe and ask, in order:

1. Are the bodies large and one-sided? Expansion.
2. Are they overlapping and drifting back into the last leg? Retracement.
3. Has a protected swing just broken with displacement? Reversal.
4. Is price stuck between two levels with no follow-through? Consolidation.

## Key terms

- **Expansion**: fast one-directional delivery that leaves gaps
- **Retracement**: a partial return into the last expansion
- **Reversal**: a change of direction confirmed by a structure shift
- **Consolidation**: a sideways range building liquidity
- **The loop**: consolidation, expansion, retracement, expansion or reversal

## Common mistakes

- **Chasing expansion.** The entry was in the retracement before it.
- **Calling a retracement a reversal.** Until the protected swing breaks with displacement, it's a pullback.
- **Trading inside consolidation.** Wait for the sweep and the expansion out of it.
- **Expecting a reversal from a range.** Ranges expand first.
