## What makes a swing point *truly* ideal?

You can now validate a swing point (Lesson 8) and use it as invalidation (Lesson 9). This lesson raises the bar from *valid* to *ideal* — the small number of setups worth the most risk.

## Non-ideal vs ideal

Both are CISDs. Only one is worth trading at full size.

| | Non-ideal | Ideal |
|---|---|---|
| **The close** | Closes over the **body** only | Closes over the **series** |

A close that clears only the body of the final opposing candle is technically a shift, but a shallow one. An **ideal** setup closes past the opening of the *entire opposing series* — every candle that participated in creating the high or low.

The wider the series cleared, the more decisively the previous delivery has been overturned.

## The simultaneity rule

This is the sentence to memorise:

> **Price has to make a Candle 2 closure while *simultaneously* creating a protected swing.**

Two conditions, in the **same** moment:

1. The **Candle 2 closure** (Lesson 5) — sweeps the extreme, closes back inside.
2. A **protected swing** (Lesson 9) forms as a result.

Not one and then the other. Both, together. The video is emphatic that treating them as separate events is *"a recipe for disaster"* — because a Candle 2 closure that does not simultaneously produce a protected swing gives you a signal with no invalidation, and a protected swing without the closure gives you invalidation with no signal.

## The formation checklist

All four must be true:

1. **Occurs on a Candle 2 or Candle 3 closure**
2. **Closes past the opposing series opening** (not just the body)
3. **Simultaneously forms a protected swing**
4. **Used with higher-timeframe Points of Interest (FVGs)**

Item four is the context requirement that has appeared in every lesson since Lesson 5. Ideal swing points happen *at* higher-timeframe levels, not anywhere.

## Avoiding fakeouts

The failure mode this guards against is the **breakout trap**: price clears a level, everyone piles in, and it immediately reverses. A fakeout produces a break with no series closure and no protected swing. An ideal setup produces both.

The checklist is the filter. Run it honestly and most breakouts get rejected — which is the intended outcome.

## The AMD entry model, applied

The complete workflow, from setup to target:

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **1. Manipulation** | A sweep of an old high/low, or a range deviation |
| **2. Ideal form** | Identify a CISD forming a protected swing |
| **3. Enter & target** | Enter on the Order Block; target standard deviations |

### Targets

Entry sits at the **Order Block** or FVG left behind by the displacement (Lesson 11). Targets run to the standard deviation projections from Lesson 7 — with the **-2 to -2.5** zone as the primary objective.

## Are your swing points actually protected?

The closing challenge is aimed at the most common self-deception in this method: taking **premature candle closures** and hoping. Hoping is the tell. If you need the next candle to cooperate for your swing point to be protected, it is not protected yet.

Professionals wait for the simultaneous protected swing. Everyone else provides the liquidity.

## Key terms

- **Ideal setup** — closes over the entire opposing *series*, not just the body
- **Non-ideal setup** — closes over the body only
- **Simultaneity rule** — Candle 2 closure and protected swing forming together
- **Displacement** — the aggressive move that leaves the order block behind
- **Standard deviation targets** — -1, -2 to -2.5, -4 to -4.5

## Common mistakes

- **Accepting a body-only close** as an ideal setup.
- **Treating the closure and the protected swing as separate events.** They must be simultaneous.
- **Acting on a premature closure.** Wait for the candle to actually close.
