## The core rule

Lesson 3 gave you targets. This lesson gives you the mechanism that decides whether price can *reach* them.

| Candle | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Small wicks, large body** | High/low formed early. Supports expansion. |
| **Large wick, small body** | Time and range were consumed forming the wick. No expansion left. |

> **A large wick does NOT support further expansion.**

The reasoning is about budget. Every candle has a finite amount of time and a typical range. If price spends most of both travelling up and then back down to form a long wick, it has spent its budget. There is nothing left for a decisive move before the close.

A small wick means the opposite: the extreme formed early, cheaply, and the rest of the period is available for expansion.

## The opposing run

The **opposing run** is the initial move from the opening price to the high or low — *in the direction opposite to your bias*.

If you are bullish, the opposing run is the dip below the open before price rises. It is the wick you will see on the finished candle. Every candle has one; the question is how big.

| Feature | Shallow run | Large run |
|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Forms early | Takes half the candle's time |
| **Implication** | Preserves energy | No energy left |
| **Realistic target** | New liquidity pools | Only back to the daily open |

A shallow opposing run is the ideal: price dipped briefly, grabbed the liquidity below, and turned with most of the period still ahead of it. A large opposing run means the move you wanted has already been consumed.

## Three ways to trade it

Ranked from safest to riskiest.

**1. Wait & Trade** *(safest)*
Wait for the reversal candle to fully close, then trade the *next* candle. You give up some of the move, but you have the completed candle as evidence.

**2. Reversal to Expansion**
Trade the candle that turns the reversal into expansion — drop to a lower timeframe, find the change in state, and ride the expansion as it begins.

**3. Trade the Wick directly** *(riskiest)*
Enter inside the large wick as it forms. You are trading against an in-progress move with no closed candle confirming anything. Highest risk, and it is the technique that most often produces a stop-out.

The general principle:

> Wait for the next candle to have a small wick, and then get on side with that expansion candle.

## Adjusting targets

Your target should change with what the candle is telling you:

| Candle type | Opposing run | Realistic target |
|---|---|---|
| Expansion | Shallow | Liquidity — new pools |
| Reversal | Large | The daily open |

When there is a large opposing run, do not hold out for a distant liquidity target. Shift focus to the open, and avoid holding for the lows.

## The filter of time

Time is the final check, and it is often the one that saves a trade.

> **Does it make sense to enter with only a fraction of the period remaining?**

If eighty percent of a daily candle has elapsed and the move you want has not started, the answer is usually no. The budget is spent.

Two rules:

- **The high or low forms early** in a candle that will expand.
- **Wait for the candle close** — the close is the filter that confirms what actually happened.

## How do we know what price will do?

We don't.

> **We just let price tell us what it's going to do.**

That is not a cop-out; it is the entire method. You do not forecast. You wait for the market to produce a shape — small wick, early extreme, clean close — that historically precedes expansion, and only then act. Everything in the rest of this course is a way of making "price told me" mechanical rather than a feeling.

## Key terms

- **Opposing run** — the initial move from the open to the high/low, against your bias
- **Shallow run** — a small, early opposing run that preserves energy
- **Large run** — an opposing run consuming much of the candle's time and range
- **Expansion candle** — small wicks, large body, moving with intent
- **Filter of time** — using time remaining in the period as a go/no-go check

## Common mistakes

- **Trading the wick as it forms.** Tempting, and the fastest route to being stopped out.
- **Keeping a distant target after a large opposing run.** The energy for that target is gone; adjust to the open.
- **Entering late in a candle.** With little time left, there is no room for the expansion you need.
