## What makes a winning trade?

This is the capstone. Everything in Modules 1–5 assembles here into one workflow you can run on any market, on any day. If you learn one page of this course by heart, make it the cheat sheet below.

## Timeframe alignment — the macro-to-micro zoom

Three charts, three distinct jobs:

| Chart | Job |
|---|---|
| **Daily** | Determine the overarching bias |
| **4-Hour** | Wait for the wick to form |
| **15-Minute** | Look for continuation to execute |

Russian dolls again: each timeframe sits inside the last. The daily says *which way*, the 4-hour says *when the pullback is done*, the 15-minute says *at what price*.

## Step 1 — Find the daily bias

On the daily chart, look for a **Candle 2 closure**:

> **Candle 2 Closure — price sweeps out the previous candle's low and closes back above it.**

That closure sets the expectation of a massive expansion higher. It is Lesson 5's pattern, used here as the top-level directional filter. No daily closure, no bias, no trade.

## Step 2 — 4-Hour confirmation

The 4-hour chart's job is to tell you the daily wick has finished forming (Lesson 14). What you are waiting for is a **CISD**:

> **CISD — price closes through the candles that made a low, to confirm a swing point.**

This lets you **watch the daily candle actually being built** in real time rather than waiting for its close. The daily bias points up; the 4-hour CISD confirms the low of that daily candle is set.

And the hard rule:

> **No CISD is a dealbreaker — no setup.**

If the 4-hour never produces the shift, there is no trade, regardless of how good the daily looked.

## Step 3 — 15-Minute execution

With bias set and the wick confirmed, drop to the 15-minute and execute on a **protected swing** (Lesson 9) — which gives the entry *and* the invalidation in one structure.

## The master cheat sheet

| Timeframe | Objective | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | Bias | Candle 2 or 3 closure |
| **4-Hour** | Confirm wick | CISD & swing |
| **15-Min** | Execute | Protected swing |

Three rows. Every concept in this course lives in one of them.

## Worked example

The video walks through a Bitcoin daily setup:

1. **Daily** — price sweeps a prior low and closes back above it: a Candle 2 closure. Bias is up.
2. **4-Hour** — wait for the wick. A CISD confirms the low is in and the delivery has shifted.
3. **15-Minute** — a protected swing forms. Enter, stop beyond the swing.
4. **Targets** — the standard deviation projections (Lesson 17) and fractal targets (Lesson 18).

The pattern is identical on any liquid market. Nothing about it is Bitcoin-specific.

## The governing rule

> **Align all timeframes in one direction.**

This is the sentence that decides most outcomes. A daily bias up, a 4-hour CISD up, and a 15-minute protected swing low all point the same way — that is alignment, and it is what you are waiting for.

When they disagree — a bullish daily but a bearish 4-hour — **there is no trade**. Not a smaller trade, not a quicker one. Disagreement between timeframes means the market has not decided, and you are being invited to guess.

## Which asset will you analyse first?

The workflow is asset-agnostic. Pick one liquid market and run the three-row cheat sheet on it every day for a month before adding a second. Depth in one market beats shallow coverage of five — you learn what its wicks and sessions actually look like.

## Key terms

- **TTFM** — TTrades Fractal Model
- **Timeframe alignment** — all three charts agreeing on direction
- **Dealbreaker** — a missing condition that cancels the trade outright
- **Master cheat sheet** — Daily bias → 4-Hour confirm → 15-Min execute

## Common mistakes

- **Taking the trade without the 4-hour CISD.** Named a dealbreaker for a reason.
- **Trading misaligned timeframes.** Disagreement is a signal to wait.
- **Jumping straight to the 15-minute chart.** Without the daily bias it is Lesson 5's context-free pattern trading.
