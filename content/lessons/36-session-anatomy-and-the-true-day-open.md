## The question this lesson answers

Lesson 13a introduced kill zones. This lesson zooms out to the whole day, names its sessions, and gives you the opening prices that the algorithm keys off. After this, time is a tool, not a background.

## Everything in New York time

Every time in this course is New York time. Not London, not your local clock. The reason is practical: the reference opens are New York opens, the US data releases are on New York time, and the community you'll read is all speaking it. Set a second clock if you have to.

## The four sessions

| Session | New York time | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Asia** | 8pm to midnight, and on through the early hours | Quiet, range-building. Accumulation. |
| **London** | 2am to 5am is the kill zone | The first real expansion, or the Judas swing |
| **New York AM** | 7am to 10am is the kill zone, 9:30 is the equity open | The highest-volume window of the day |
| **London close / NY PM** | 10am to noon, then 1:30pm to 4pm | Reversals into the London close, and the afternoon expansion |

A rough script for a normal day, in Power of Three terms: Asia accumulates, London manipulates or starts to distribute, New York delivers the bulk of the distribution, and the afternoon either extends it or reverses into the close.

## The kill zones inside them

You don't trade a session. You trade the window inside it when the algorithm is actually delivering:

- **London kill zone**: 2am to 5am
- **New York AM kill zone**: 7am to 10am
- **London close kill zone**: 10am to noon
- **New York PM kill zone**: 1:30pm to 4pm

Outside those windows, the arrays from Module 7 are just levels. Inside them, they're setups. That's the rule from Lesson 29, and this is where it comes from.

## The two opens

Power of Three needs an open to measure from. There are two that matter.

> **The midnight open (the true day open).** The price at 12:00am New York. The day's accumulation forms around it, and the day's manipulation is measured against it.

> **The 8:30 open.** The price at 8:30am New York, when the day's data releases land and the New York session gets going.

Use them exactly the way Lesson 34 described the open: on a bullish day, buys are better below the midnight open, and a New York buy is better below the 8:30 open. Being above the open in a bullish bias means you're paying premium for the day.

Mark both as horizontal lines every day. They cost nothing and they orient everything else.

## The day, as a plan

1. Before London: mark the Asian range, the previous day's high and low, the midnight open, and the daily bias.
2. London kill zone: watch for the Judas swing against the bias, then the shift. First entry.
3. Between London and New York: usually a retracement. Manage, don't add.
4. New York AM kill zone: mark the 8:30 open. Watch for the second manipulation at 9:30. Second entry, or the continuation.
5. London close: if the day has run a long way, expect a reversal or a deep retracement here.
6. Afternoon: the last expansion into the close, or nothing. Never force the afternoon.

That plan repeats every day. The details change, the shape rarely does.

## Days that don't behave

Some days are all consolidation: no expansion in London, none in New York. Those are usually the days before high-impact news, which Lesson 39 covers. Others expand early in Asia and spend the rest of the day retracing. The plan above is for the typical day. When the day is clearly not typical by 10am, the right trade is often none.

## Key terms

- **The four sessions**: Asia, London, New York AM, London close / NY PM
- **Kill zone**: the delivery window inside a session
- **Midnight open**: the true day open, 12:00am New York
- **8:30 open**: the New York reference open

## Common mistakes

- **Working in your local time.** Everything is New York.
- **Trading the session instead of the kill zone.** The windows are narrow on purpose.
- **Forgetting to mark the opens.** They're the reference for premium and discount on the day.
- **Forcing the afternoon.** It's the lowest-probability window for most people.
