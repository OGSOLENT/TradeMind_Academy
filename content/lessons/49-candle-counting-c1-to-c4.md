## Why count candles at all

You've traded the Candle 2 closure (Lesson 5) and the Candle 3 variant (Lesson 6). You've used the T-Spot (Lesson 16) and the playbook (Lesson 19). What I want to do now is line those pieces up so you can see they're one sequence, not four tricks. The Fractal Model is a count. Four candles, on whatever timeframe you're reading, and each one has a job.

The reason a count works is this:

> **The market cannot reverse without forming a swing high or a swing low.**

That sounds obvious until you notice what it gives you. If a reversal has to make a swing, and a swing takes three candles to form (a candle, a candle that pokes past it, and a candle that closes back), then every reversal announces itself over three closes. You don't need to guess the turn. You need to count to three and then read what the third close says.

## The four candles

Here's the count on a bullish reversal, so the swing is a swing low.

> **Candle 1.** The candle that sets the level. Its low is the low that's about to be run.

Candle 1 is nothing special on its own. It's just the most recent candle, and its low is sitting there with stops beneath it. You only know it was Candle 1 in hindsight, when Candle 2 does what Candle 2 does.

> **Candle 2.** The reversal candle. It runs below Candle 1's low and closes back above it.

This is the Candle 2 closure from Lesson 5. The sweep is the manipulation; the close back inside is the tell. On this candle the swing low is formed, and it's the low of Candle 2 that becomes the swing.

> **Candle 3.** The confirmation candle. It closes above Candle 2's high, or at least holds above Candle 2's equilibrium and closes strong.

Candle 3 is where most of the money is made or lost, and I'll come back to it. If Candle 2 was the turn, Candle 3 is the first candle that has to prove it by expanding away from the swing.

> **Candle 4.** The continuation candle. It expands out of the upper half of Candle 3's range.

Candle 4 is the one TTrades calls one of the most powerful opportunities in the model, and I agree, for a reason that's easy to miss: by the time Candle 4 opens, the swing is confirmed, the direction is confirmed, and you're no longer guessing. You're joining.

| Candle | Job | What confirms it |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Sets the level | Nothing yet. It's just the previous candle. |
| C2 | Reverses: sweeps C1's extreme, closes back inside | The close. A wick through the level that closes back is the pattern; a close through it is not. |
| C3 | Confirms: expands away from the swing | A close beyond C2's high (bullish) or low (bearish), holding above C2's midpoint on the way |
| C4 | Continues: expands from the upper (or lower) half of C3 | Opening in C3's top half and not trading deep into it |

## The Candle 3 equilibrium rule

This is the rule I'd write on the wall.

> **Candle 3's equilibrium is critical. Expansions do not tolerate deep retracements.**

Take the halfway point of Candle 3's range. In a bullish sequence, Candle 4 should open in the upper half and stay there. If Candle 4 trades down through Candle 3's midpoint, the expansion is already in trouble, and if it closes below it, the count is broken. A real expansion is impatient. It pulls back shallow, into the top of the previous candle, and goes again. A move that comes all the way back to the middle of the previous candle isn't expanding; it's ranging, and the model has nothing to say about ranges.

So the rule does two things at once. It tells you where Candle 4 is allowed to trade (upper half of Candle 3), and it gives you a clean invalidation (a close below Candle 3's 50%). You'll notice this is the same idea as the mean threshold on an order block from Lesson 11 and the 50% of the leg in the four phases (Lesson 27). Equilibrium keeps turning up because it's the line between a pullback and a failure.

## Where Candle 4 trades from

"Expands out of the upper half of Candle 3" has a practical meaning on the lower timeframe. Inside Candle 4, you're looking for:

1. The open to sit in Candle 3's upper half.
2. A shallow dip, ideally into Candle 3's high or a fair value gap left by Candle 3's expansion, not below the midpoint.
3. A lower-timeframe CISD (Lesson 7) forming a protected low at that dip.
4. Expansion away from it, through Candle 3's high, towards the targets you set with the projections in Lesson 17.

If step 2 goes wrong and the dip is deep, don't force step 3. A CISD that forms below Candle 3's midpoint is confirming a different story.

## The same count, the other way

For a bearish reversal, flip every word. Candle 1 sets a high. Candle 2 runs above it and closes back below. Candle 3 closes below Candle 2's low. Candle 4 expands out of the lower half of Candle 3. Equilibrium is still the line; it's just that now price has to stay under it.

## The count is fractal

Nothing about this depends on the timeframe. A daily Candle 2 is made of 24 hourly candles, and somewhere in those hourly candles there's an hourly Candle 2, 3 and 4 that built the daily one. That's why the model is called fractal, and it's why the timeframe pairs in Lesson 51 matter: you count on the higher timeframe and you enter on the lower one.

## Key terms

- **Candle 1**: the candle whose extreme gets swept
- **Candle 2**: the reversal candle; sweeps Candle 1 and closes back inside
- **Candle 3**: the confirmation candle; expands away from the new swing
- **Candle 4**: the continuation candle; expands from the upper (or lower) half of Candle 3
- **Candle 3 equilibrium**: the midpoint of Candle 3's range, the line a real expansion doesn't close through
- **Swing formation**: the three-candle sequence that every reversal has to make

## Common mistakes

- **Counting from the wrong candle.** Candle 1 is the candle that gets swept. If nothing was swept, you don't have a Candle 2 yet.
- **Accepting a close through the level as Candle 2.** A Candle 2 closes back inside. A close beyond is continuation, not reversal.
- **Ignoring Candle 3's midpoint.** A Candle 4 that trades through it has told you the expansion is failing.
- **Trading Candle 4 from the wrong half.** From the top half, with a shallow dip, it's a continuation. From the bottom half it's a coin flip.
