## The question this lesson answers

So far every model has been a moment: a sweep, a gap, a window. The market maker model is the shape of a whole day or week of price, from the range it starts in to the range it ends in. It's the big picture that all the small entries live inside.

The name comes from the idea that a market maker's job is to fill orders, and price has to travel to wherever the orders are. The credits in this curriculum call it the MMXM, and it's the same model your daily bias videos were built on.

## The shape

> **Price leaves a consolidation, travels to a pool of liquidity, reverses there, and travels back through the consolidation to the liquidity on the other side.**

The buy model, stage by stage:

| Stage | What happens | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Original consolidation** | Price ranges. Positions get built. | The staging area |
| 2. **The sell-side of the curve** | Price expands *down* out of the range in legs, each with a small retracement | Engineering the sellside liquidity below |
| 3. **Smart money reversal** | Price sweeps the low it was heading for and shifts structure back up | The turn |
| 4. **The buy-side of the curve** | Price travels back up through the consolidation in legs | The intended delivery |
| 5. **Buyside liquidity** | Price reaches the high it was really heading for all along | The target |

The sell model is the mirror: consolidation, legs up to run the buyside, reversal, legs down through the consolidation to the sellside.

Draw it and you get a curve, down and then up, symmetrical around the reversal. The left side of the curve is manipulation on a large scale. The right side is distribution. It's Power of Three, stretched across the whole move.

## Reading the two sides

**The left side** is a trap. Every leg down looks like a trend, and every retracement looks like a place to short. The tell that you're on the left side is that the legs are heading *toward* a clean pool of liquidity that hasn't been taken yet, and the higher-timeframe bias is against the direction of travel.

**The reversal** is the single most important moment in the model, and it's the 2022 model from Lesson 40 exactly: sweep of the low, shift up with displacement, gap.

**The right side** is where you make the money. Each retracement on the way back up is a re-entry, and the levels those retracements respect are the ones the left side left behind: the order blocks and gaps from the legs down, now flipped, which is where Module 7's breakers came from.

## Where your entries sit

| Entry model | Where in the MMXM |
|---|---|
| Turtle soup | The reversal, entered on the sweep |
| The 2022 model | The reversal, entered on the gap after the shift |
| Breaker / unicorn | The first retracement on the right side |
| Mitigation block | Later retracements on the right side |
| Silver Bullet | Any of those, if it lands in the hour |

Nothing in this course is a separate system. Everything is a place on this curve.

## The consolidation is the key

The original consolidation matters more than it looks. Its midpoint, its equilibrium, is the level the return leg has to cross. When price on the right side gets back to the consolidation, expect a reaction there: it's the biggest zone of resting orders on the chart. Often the first real retracement of the return leg happens at the consolidation's edge, and the continuation resumes after it.

## Timeframes

The model shows up on every timeframe, but it's clearest on the 15-minute to 1-hour for the intraday version, and on the 4-hour to daily for the weekly one. The weekly profiles in Module 13 are market maker models seen through the calendar: the classic expansion week is a whole buy or sell model completed in five days.

## Key terms

- **Market maker model (MMXM)**: the full curve from consolidation to liquidity and back
- **Original consolidation**: the range the move starts in
- **Smart money reversal**: the sweep and shift at the far end of the curve
- **Left side / right side**: engineering the liquidity, then delivering to the other pool

## Common mistakes

- **Trading the left side as a trend.** It's the manipulation.
- **Ignoring the consolidation on the way back.** It's the biggest zone on the chart.
- **Expecting symmetry to the tick.** The shape is symmetrical in stages, not in size.
- **Treating the model as an entry.** It's a map. The entries are the models you already have.
