## The reversal foundation

> **The market cannot reverse without a swing point.**

Simple, and absolute. Every reversal — on every timeframe, in every market — requires a swing point to reverse *from*. If you cannot identify the swing point, you are not looking at a reversal; you are looking at a move you have not understood yet.

This is why Module 4 came first. The swing point is the atom.

## OHLC as a sequence

> **OHLC — the sequence of Open, High, Low and Close for a given timeframe candle.**

Lesson 1 taught OHLC as four data points. Here it becomes a **sequence in time** — the order in which those points are made, which is the whole story of the candle:

**Open → Manipulation up to the High → Aggressive run lower → Close near the bottom.**

That is an OHLC (bearish) day from Lesson 13, described as a process. Reading the sequence rather than the shape lets you *anticipate tomorrow* rather than describe yesterday. If you know the high forms early on a bearish day, you know to wait for it rather than chase the open.

## Confirming the shift

Three steps, which by now should feel familiar:

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **1. Sweep** | Sweep the previous candle's high or low |
| **2. Close** | Close back inside the range |
| **3. Shift** | Change in the State of Delivery |

Steps 1–2 are the Candle 2 closure (Lesson 5). Step 3 is the CISD (Lesson 7). The fractal model does not introduce a new pattern — it formalises the ones you have into a single repeatable unit.

### Delivery shift, defined precisely

> **Delivery shift — closing through the specific candles that originally created the high or low.**

Not any candles. *The ones that made the extreme.* Those are the candles whose participants must be overcome for the direction to genuinely change.

## The T-Spot

The model's signature idea:

> **T-Spot — the target area where a higher-timeframe wick forms before continuing the trend.**

And the intention behind it:

> **We are actively anticipating the higher-timeframe wick before the true expansion.**

Every higher-timeframe candle forms a wick before it expands — the opposing run (Lesson 4), the daily wick (Lesson 14). The T-Spot is the *zone where that wick is expected to form*.

Anticipating it means you are waiting at the price where the market is likely to dip before running, rather than chasing after it has already gone. This is the difference between entering at the start of the expansion and entering halfway through it.

## Going fractal

The model is scale-independent:

> **Since my model is fractal... I can apply this on multiple timeframes.**

The powerful application is **overlapping a higher-timeframe T-Spot with a lower-timeframe setup**:

1. Identify the T-Spot on the higher timeframe — *where* price should react.
2. Wait for price to reach it.
3. Drop to the lower timeframe and look for the full setup — sweep, close, shift — *inside* that zone.

Higher timeframe gives location; lower timeframe gives confirmation and a tight stop. Neither alone is enough: a T-Spot with no lower-timeframe confirmation is a hopeful level, and a lower-timeframe setup with no T-Spot is Lesson 5's context-free pattern.

## Key terms

- **T-Spot** — the zone where a higher-timeframe wick is expected to form
- **Delivery shift** — closing through the candles that created the extreme
- **Fractal** — the same model applied at any timeframe
- **Kill zone** — a time window limiting when setups are valid

## Common mistakes

- **Looking for reversals without a swing point.** By definition there isn't one.
- **Trading the T-Spot on arrival** without a lower-timeframe confirmation.
- **Closing through the wrong candles.** The delivery shift is specifically through the candles that made the extreme.
